# Facety simulator backlog

Scope: a reproducible research simulator for [the proposed protocol](docs/facety.protocol.en.md) (Rust, [`simulator/`](simulator)), not a production runtime or proof of personhood. This file alone owns open work. Meaning lives in the protocol, decisions in the [decision log](docs/decisions.en.md), findings in the [insight register](docs/insights.en.md#consolidated-findings), outcomes in [CHANGELOG](CHANGELOG.md), assumptions and owner questions in [DECISIONS_NEEDED](DECISIONS_NEEDED.md). The pre-consolidation backlog (full narrative, parked work packages) is at [`legacy-node`](https://github.com/llblab/facety/blob/legacy-node/BACKLOG.md).

**Current priority (D129, D132):** show with the Rust simulator, up to a year or two of network growth, that the topology defense keeps the dirty-FACY share at most 10% and the clean share at least two thirds, at a bearable cost to honest people. Detection is always on; its design is the variable.

**Phase (2026-11-08): finite evidence-core closure on D162-D167.** The formed rules use overlapping monotonic deadlines, maturity XP, integrity, soft backing and debt service, anyone joining, simple-majority Council and no Treasury. `examiner_looks = 2` is an uncalibrated behavior assumption. Q20-Q24 are answered; the earlier K programme is historical evidence under earlier mechanics. The current task closes repeat-observation dependence, realized costs/recovery and dispute completion, runs one compact joint confirmation, delivers a verdict and stops. No broad benchmark or runtime/pilot implementation is authorized. The [latest study](studies/2026-11-07-maturity-and-ladder/README.md) preserves B4's crossed kill line.

## Core closure programme (opened 2026-11-06, finite)

**Goal:** one rule set, one executable model, reproducible evidence, stated limits and a decision about the next stage; then the research loop stops. **Candidate core:** the mechanics baseline of the axis table with the selected rules. The variants of Q20 (verification before a case, softer D131) and every other opt-in lever are sensitivity checks only, because an exploratory variant is not a protocol rule. Criteria are the accepted ones (D129: dirty-FACY share at most 10% and clean share at least two thirds at the longest declared horizon). No honest-damage tolerance is assigned here: the honest network is compared with a paired attack-free control on the same seeds, and a line at two thirds of the control is only a reporting line borrowed from the clean-share criterion, not an owner tolerance. Every result is diagnostic (no registration tooling); the one confirmation series uses reserved seeds `grown-1001..1012` and criteria written below before it runs.

- [x] Statistical validation against the retired simulator ([contract](docs/simulator.en.md#baseline-validation-statistical)). Node simulator, tests and raw artifacts deleted at the tag `legacy-node`.
- [x] **Scale runs under the baseline (2026-11-06).** One year with 10, 30 and 100 operators, a patient supported-first attacker, an honest-first attacker (12 to 20 seeds each) and two years with 30 operators (3 seeds): the cone never forms, the attacker share is the operators divided by the honest accounts (0.09% to 0.95% of accounts, at most 1.5% of FACY), far inside the D129 criterion. Tables: [simulator contract](docs/simulator.en.md#baseline-validation-statistical).
- [x] **Registration tooling (dropped, 2026-11-06).** The closure programme used criteria and a frozen adversary committed before the runs and reserved seeds; a runner mode adds no research value for the core decision. Every result stays labeled diagnostic.
- [x] **Simulator efficiency (D138, closed as a standing item on 2026-11-06; reopen only when a run that the core needs is blocked).** Done at the owner's request (2026-11-06): memory was dominated by the records of emission acts (about 490 bytes per act: guarantor list, one receipt per recipient and an index of XP by pair); the guarantor and recipient lists are now shared between consecutive acts of an account, revoked receipts are a sparse list, and the XP index is gone (revocation scans the acts of the guaranteed account). Outcomes are identical (result files of six runs covering growth, a cone without layered support, key loss, bought accounts, false accusations, appeals, restoration, XP penalties and the subtree burden compare equal before and after). Heavy seed (100 operators, supported-first, 120 days of patience, 20% bought accounts), one thread: one year 208 s and 1.4 GB before, 159 s and 0.28 GB after; two years 1,056 s and 5.3 GB before, 908 s and 0.68 GB after. Remaining ideas, each to be checked for identical outcomes: the superlinear time of the second year, per-subject act storage. Done: exact two-sided pruning of the path search (30%-40% faster in the heavy mode); the end-of-run `audit()` made linear instead of quadratic in accounts (58 s to 2 s for four seeds at 3000 honest accounts, outputs byte-identical), which turned a year of growth from impossible into about 50 s per seed. Efficiency settings are a baseline of their own (D145): they never change outcomes, and a faster mechanism becomes the default where it helps. Done: the search order among equal distances is a setting with four values (`--search-order none|xp|degree|both`, the owner's ideas of XP and of the number of guarantors, alone and together); measured steps: heavy ablation without layered support none 986M, XP 820M (best, 17% fewer, 20% fewer lost searches), degree 949M, both 903M (as the product of XP and the number of guarantors, the owner's refinement; the sum gave 906M); baseline with layered support none 126k (best), XP 301k, degree 834k, both 801k (product; the sum gave 312k); at a year and 10,000 accounts the whole search is only 130k steps, so the order cannot matter there. The automatic default is `degree` (more mutual guarantors first) for every rule set, by owner decision; the measured step counts above favor XP without layered support and none with layered support, so the ordering is a candidate to revisit only if the exact search becomes hot again. Path-discovery study (identical snapshots, `simulator/tests/path_probe.rs`): with layered support a verified structural certificate (D descending chains read off the support levels, checked against the live graph) or a proof of too few first hops settles 99.5% of the questions (6,801 of 6,834) with 240 search steps instead of 126,873 and 7.7 microseconds per question instead of 116 (15 times faster), identical answers, no invalid certificate; it is the default under layered support. A bounded-flow relaxation (time-expanded, proves impossibility) settles 17% of the impossible searches in the ablation (7.4M steps fall to 5.6M, exhausted searches 10 to 9) but costs as much as it saves (467 against 450 microseconds) and never fires under layered support, so it stays off. The question itself is cheap at the baseline (about 5% of a run); the real cost at scale is not the search but the honest behavior: friend-of-friend selection now uses a stamp array and a partial selection instead of sorting and deduplicating lists, and integer-keyed tables use a fast hasher; together about three times faster per seed (34-40 s to 12-14 s for 250 days), with byte-identical results; the two-sided structure already exists (distances from the Council are cached once a day and shared, distances from the issuer are computed per issuer). Next ideas: no-good memoization and negative caches per issuer and epoch in the path search, incremental support levels, bitset adjacency, struct-of-arrays instead of per-account heap allocations, threads inside one run for the daily detection pass. Every change must leave outcomes identical per seed (compare saved results before and after) or declare the deviation in the simulator contract.

## Topology defense

- [x] **Growth stalls near 10,000 active accounts (closed as a finding, D148; qualified by D153: the false-positive dispute load is a main cause as well, see Detection precision).** Honest growth reaches about 10,100 accounts and stays there while D flips between 4 and 5: five disjoint paths of at most seven nodes to five different seats of the 11-seat Council (D33: `G = round(sqrt(D))` digits, 1, zeros, 1) are rarely available at that size. The owner confirmed the Council size is fixed (D148; the proposal D146 is withdrawn), so the plateau is a consequence of the selected rule, about 1,000 honest accounts per seat at D = 5. Diagnostic ablations (500 days, 4 seeds, honest accounts at the end; larger Councils are not protocol options): baseline 10,482; path bound 9: 12,018; D capped at 4: 12,422; K = 300: 10,673; the Council-size map (21 seats 16,724 up to 51 seats 66,592, with the attacker share falling from 0.95% to 0.15%) is in the [study](studies/2026-10-30-grown-network/README.md#growth-against-security-council-size-d144-diagnostic). Remaining levers (D144): path bound, difficulty cap, slot ceiling. K by XP level (D147) was checked against the plateau and does not move it (see the [study](studies/2026-10-30-grown-network/README.md#slot-ceiling-by-xp-level-d147-diagnostic)).

- [x] **Detection precision (closed, 2026-11-06).** Done: appeal and restoration (D151), verification before a case (D153), correlated examiner errors and proof-window sensitivity (K2). Dropped for lack of research interest: escalation thresholds and per-account false-positive budgets (less attention already improves the honest network at equal containment, so the open question is the owner's Q20, not another threshold). Open only as owner questions Q20 and Q21.
- [x] **Stronger attackers (closed, 2026-11-06).** Done: patient operators, real-person accomplices (the operators), bought honest accounts up to 50% (D154), false accusations (K3), cash-out, and a frozen strongest adversary confirmed on reserved seeds (K5). Dropped: detection-aware evasion (under layered support no cone forms, so there is nothing to evade; it becomes relevant only for a network without layered support) and the price of buying links (an economic sweep with no owner valuation; attacks stay unpriced and profitability is not claimed).
- [x] **Honest behavior sensitivity (done, 2026-11-06).** Result: the lines hold in all twelve configurations; see the [study](studies/2026-11-06-core-closure/README.md#honest-behavior-regimes-backlog-item-diagnostic). Original plan:  The honest model already has friend-of-friend closure, invitation trees and careless accounts. Remaining test for the core verdict: do the D129 lines and the honest-to-control ratio survive other connectivity regimes (befriend, invite and stranger-acceptance rates sparse and dense)? One-factor sweep, 30 and 100 operators, 4 seeds; success if the lines hold in all regimes, refutation names the regime. Calibrated human behavior stays blocked on data about people.
- [x] **Second accounts (D152).** Done: the acquittal needs a co-presence artifact (`--artifact`, `--collusion`); artifact for everyone keeps 7% more honest accounts and removes the cascade, the holder is invalidated at the same rate, and a collusion share passes into the acquitted share ([study](studies/2026-10-30-grown-network/README.md#second-accounts-with-a-co-presence-artifact-d152-diagnostic)). The collusion leak is priced as an estimate (about 175 of 431 kept links belong to colluders at 50% collusion); the XP penalty of D34/D55 for inviters now exists (`--xp-penalty`) and acquitted inviters are exempt.
- [x] **Remedy for a detected cluster (D151, closed as a diagnostic).** Built and measured: the burden of proof on the subtree (`--subtree-cases`, halves the cluster without layered support, neutral under the layered baseline), appeal (`--appeal`, +5% honest accounts with a poor detector), fee-free link restoration (`--restore-links`, no benefit shown), liquid recovery (`--recover-liquid`, negligible: fakes hold almost no liquid FACY), the proof window (7/14/28 days, no monotone effect, seven days kept), and the D34/D55 XP penalty for the inviter (`--xp-penalty`, no effect on the cluster in the model). Tables in the [study](studies/2026-10-30-grown-network/README.md#burden-of-proof-on-the-invitation-subtree-d151-diagnostic). Left only as research: the deterrent value of the penalty needs modeled incentives, and the real value of recovered FACY needs attackers that hold liquid FACY.
- [x] **Incentive models for the D151 and D152 remedies (valuation sweeps, diagnostic).** Built: the double-life ledger and a declared cash-out behavior of operators (`--cash-out`); the [study](studies/2026-10-30-grown-network/README.md#incentive-analysis-of-the-inviter-penalties-and-of-liquid-recovery-d151-d152-diagnostic-valuation-sweeps) sweeps the valuation instead of choosing a utility. Findings: the inviter's loss deters when one emission is worth less than about 0.5 to 2 links depending on how XP is valued, acquittal on a fabricated artifact removes it entirely, and liquid recovery removes 16%-29% of the dirty FACY of cashing-out operators. Left as research: the authenticity and freshness of the artifact (D152). The owner decided to keep the sweep and not to replace it with a point estimate, so the valuation stays a swept parameter.

- [x] **Social recovery scenario (D135, done, 2026-11-06).** See the [study](studies/2026-11-06-core-closure/README.md#key-loss-and-social-recovery-d135-scenario-diagnostic): works, slow and incomplete; the bottleneck of the funnel is not isolated (ceiling and vouching probability excluded).
