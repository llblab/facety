This file alone owns open work. Meaning lives in the protocol, decisions in the [decision log](docs/decisions.en.md), findings in the [insight register](docs/insights.en.md#consolidated-findings), outcomes in [CHANGELOG](CHANGELOG.md), assumptions and owner questions in [DECISIONS_NEEDED](DECISIONS_NEEDED.md). The pre-consolidation backlog (full narrative, parked work packages) is at [`legacy-node`](https://github.com/llblab/facety/blob/legacy-node/BACKLOG.md).

**Current priority (D129, D132):** show with the Rust simulator, up to a year or two of network growth, that the topology defense keeps the dirty-FACY share at most 10% and the clean share at least two thirds, at a bearable cost to honest people. Detection is always on; its design is the variable.

**Phase (D140): baseline complete, awaiting owner feedback.** Every axis below has a named value; the last three were proposed by the builder (D143) and the owner may overrule them. From here a broad benchmark and a search for pits at particular values make sense. Order of work (D141): stability, analysis, understanding, representativeness.

## Rust simulator

- [x] **Correctness and trust pass (2026-11-06).** Single-use social recovery with a strengthened audit; one formal-case procedure with the XP gate (§25) for every route; saved results with the full configuration, schema, engine, randomness policy, build commit and phases, valid JSON, no silent overwrite; strict parameter and argument validation; `cargo fmt --check`, `cargo clippy -D warnings` and `cargo test` green. Statistics of earlier runs are not comparable (see the simulator contract).
- [ ] **Recovery and dynamics (D141, after the baseline).** Measure how the system keeps and restores the honest community after errors (regrowth after false-positive shocks, restoration of wrongly invalidated accounts, time to recover) and show time series (attacker share, dirty-FACY share, honest accounts) as SVG or HTML graphs instead of single-day endpoints. Order of work: stability, analysis, understanding, representativeness.

- [x] Core: graph, keyed randomness, exact length-bounded disjoint paths with verifier, layered support, world rules, runner, snapshot cloning, tests, documentation integrity guard.
- [x] Statistical validation against the retired simulator ([contract](docs/simulator.en.md#baseline-validation-statistical)). Node simulator, tests and raw artifacts deleted at the tag `legacy-node`.
- [x] **Scale runs under the baseline (2026-11-06).** One year with 10, 30 and 100 operators, a patient supported-first attacker, an honest-first attacker (12 to 20 seeds each) and two years with 30 operators (3 seeds): the cone never forms, the attacker share is the operators divided by the honest accounts (0.09% to 0.95% of accounts, at most 1.5% of FACY), far inside the D129 criterion. Tables: [simulator contract](docs/simulator.en.md#baseline-validation-statistical).
- [ ] **Registration tooling.** A committed registration (claim, presence, necessity by ablation, named alternatives, kill criterion, reserved seeds) and a runner mode that refuses changed implementations; until then every result is diagnostic.
- [ ] **Standing: simulator efficiency (D138).** Never closed. Done: exact two-sided pruning of the path search (30%-40% faster in the heavy mode); the end-of-run `audit()` made linear instead of quadratic in accounts (58 s to 2 s for four seeds at 3000 honest accounts, outputs byte-identical), which turned a year of growth from impossible into about 50 s per seed. Efficiency settings are a baseline of their own (D145): they never change outcomes, and a faster mechanism becomes the default where it helps. Done: the search order among equal distances is a setting with four values (`--search-order none|xp|degree|both`, the owner's ideas of XP and of the number of guarantors, alone and together); measured steps: heavy ablation without layered support none 986M, XP 820M (best, 17% fewer, 20% fewer lost searches), degree 949M, both 903M (as the product of XP and the number of guarantors, the owner's refinement; the sum gave 906M); baseline with layered support none 126k (best), XP 301k, degree 834k, both 801k (product; the sum gave 312k); at a year and 10,000 accounts the whole search is only 130k steps, so the order cannot matter there. The automatic default is `degree` (more mutual guarantors first) for every rule set, by owner decision; the measured step counts above favor XP without layered support and none with layered support, so the ordering is a candidate to revisit only if the exact search becomes hot again. Path-discovery study (identical snapshots, `simulator/tests/path_probe.rs`): with layered support a verified structural certificate (D descending chains read off the support levels, checked against the live graph) or a proof of too few first hops settles 99.5% of the questions (6,801 of 6,834) with 240 search steps instead of 126,873 and 7.7 microseconds per question instead of 116 (15 times faster), identical answers, no invalid certificate; it is the default under layered support. A bounded-flow relaxation (time-expanded, proves impossibility) settles 17% of the impossible searches in the ablation (7.4M steps fall to 5.6M, exhausted searches 10 to 9) but costs as much as it saves (467 against 450 microseconds) and never fires under layered support, so it stays off. The question itself is cheap at the baseline (about 5% of a run); the real cost at scale is not the search but the honest behavior: friend-of-friend selection now uses a stamp array and a partial selection instead of sorting and deduplicating lists, and integer-keyed tables use a fast hasher; together about three times faster per seed (34-40 s to 12-14 s for 250 days), with byte-identical results; the two-sided structure already exists (distances from the Council are cached once a day and shared, distances from the issuer are computed per issuer). Next ideas: no-good memoization and negative caches per issuer and epoch in the path search, incremental support levels, bitset adjacency, struct-of-arrays instead of per-account heap allocations, threads inside one run for the daily detection pass. Every change must leave outcomes identical per seed (compare saved results before and after) or declare the deviation in the simulator contract.

## Topology defense

- [x] **Growth stalls near 10,000 active accounts (closed as a finding, D148).** Honest growth reaches about 10,100 accounts and stays there while D flips between 4 and 5: five disjoint paths of at most seven nodes to five different seats of the 11-seat Council (D33: `G = round(sqrt(D))` digits, 1, zeros, 1) are rarely available at that size. The owner confirmed the Council size is fixed (D148; the proposal D146 is withdrawn), so the plateau is a consequence of the selected rule, about 1,000 honest accounts per seat at D = 5. Diagnostic ablations (500 days, 4 seeds, honest accounts at the end; larger Councils are not protocol options): baseline 10,482; path bound 9: 12,018; D capped at 4: 12,422; K = 300: 10,673; the Council-size map (21 seats 16,724 up to 51 seats 66,592, with the attacker share falling from 0.95% to 0.15%) is in the [study](studies/2026-10-30-grown-network/README.md#growth-against-security-council-size-d144-diagnostic). Remaining levers (D144): path bound, difficulty cap, slot ceiling. Next: K by XP level (D147) with a level size taken from the recorded XP quantiles (`--k-step 50 --k-xp-per-level`), checked against the plateau.

- [ ] **Detection precision.** The false-positive rate and the inspection rate decide honest cost (insight 5, 6). Design and compare: verification before opening a case, escalation thresholds, appeal and fee-free restoration of honest links, false-positive budgets per account.
- [ ] **Stronger attackers.** Patient or sleeper operators, real-person accomplices (D118) at 30-100 operators, detection-aware behavior, buying honest links systematically, compromised honest accounts.
- [ ] **Honest behavior realism.** Replace uniform acceptance rates with deliberate and careless acceptance, correlated friendships and cautious strangers' vetting with calibrated look quality.
- [x] **Second accounts (D152).** Done: the acquittal needs a co-presence artifact (`--artifact`, `--collusion`); artifact for everyone keeps 7% more honest accounts and removes the cascade, the holder is invalidated at the same rate, and a collusion share passes into the acquitted share ([study](studies/2026-10-30-grown-network/README.md#second-accounts-with-a-co-presence-artifact-d152-diagnostic)). Left: price the collusion leak (what a colluding pair gains) and extend to the XP penalties of D34/D55 once the runner applies them to inviters.
- [ ] **Remedy for a detected cluster (D151).** Done: the burden of proof on the invitation subtree with a partial cap (`--subtree-cases`) halves the cluster without layered support (26.3% to 15.3% of accounts, dirty-FACY share 0.8% to 0.4%, no honest cost; [study](studies/2026-10-30-grown-network/README.md#burden-of-proof-on-the-invitation-subtree-d151-diagnostic)). Left: appeal and fee-free restoration of honest links cut by false positives, recovery of liquid FACY, the proof window, and a run against the layered baseline with 100 operators.
- [ ] **Protocol text.** D123, D131, D133 and D134 are written in §46.14-§46.15; D124 stays a proposal until adopted. The retired first-100 exception (D91) was removed from the summary tables, §18, §20, §34 and §46; D89 is marked superseded.
- [ ] **Model the new concepts.** Social recovery (D135): the world-level operations and tests exist; still to do are runner scenarios (key-loss rate, recovery success and time, abuse by colluding former guarantors) and design variants. Identification reputation (D137): attestations weighted by closeness and independence, not modeled.
