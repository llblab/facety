# Insight register

This register keeps every design insight and research finding in one place. A **decision** comes from the protocol owner and is recorded in the [protocol](facety.protocol.en.md) with its label. A **finding** comes from the simulator and states its evidence level:

- **Confirmed:** supported by a preregistered test on reserved seeds ([evidence](evidence.en.md)).
- **Exploratory:** a grid, screen or game on one seed batch. It is a map, not a result.
- **Diagnostic:** a small targeted probe that explains a mechanism.

All findings are conditional on the simulator's declared assumptions, horizons and valuations. None is a real-world measurement. Update this file whenever a decision is made or a study ends.

## Consolidated findings

**Current conclusion (2026-11-08, D168): REVISE operational assumptions before implementation/pilot; Grow Loop stopped.** Item 30 and the [final report](../studies/2026-11-08-evidence-core/README.md) own the current evidence. Items 2-29 retain their original builds and assumptions, not current-HEAD validation. Item 21's funds barrier was superseded by D166, item 22's collateral figures are nominal under soft backing, and item 28 predates D167 restrictions (no debt ceiling is selected). B4 remains killed; its growth ratio cannot establish bounded debt. D155's earlier readiness recommendation below is historical and superseded for the next-stage decision, without deleting its evidence. Selected rules are unchanged.

These are no longer hypotheses: they are stated once, with their evidence level, and the exploratory entries that led to them were folded here (D132). Every item is conditional on the declared world of the [simulator contract](simulator.en.md): a grown network of 1000 accounts, difficulty D = 4, an 11-seat Council, honest behavior at the stated rates, 10 cooperating operators unless noted, and detection on. Detail and tables: [grown-network study](../studies/2026-10-30-grown-network/README.md). Items 2 to 8 were first measured with the rule set before D131, D133, D134 and D136 (see the study page for the current baseline); the ordering of the rules, and the finding that detection precision is the main honest cost (item 5), changed in size, not in direction: with cheap proof of reality (D136) invalidated genuine accounts fall from about 57 to about 3 per 60 days, so the remaining honest cost is the time spent in spurious disputes and the paths they remove (D131). Evidence level for all items below is diagnostic (seeds are samples of one scenario family, not reserved confirmations).

1. **Detection is part of every attack simulation (obvious once stated).** With no detection nothing opposes a cluster of fakes, so a no-detection arm carries no information. The research variable is the design of detection, not its presence. (D125, [AGENTS](../AGENTS.md))
2. **Width and depth alone do not stop a cluster.** A guarantor count of D and D disjoint paths of at most 7 nodes leave 9% to 43% of accounts to 10 operators after 60 days (mean 25% over 20 seeds; 60% with no bound, one seed). Vertex-disjointness is counted per issuer, so thousands of cone members share the same few bridges to the honest network.
3. **The 7-node bound costs honest growth nothing and is justified by verification cost, not by security.** Days to 1000 honest accounts are identical with and without it on 12 seeds; 5 nodes also cost nothing, 4 nodes about 4 days and 3 nodes 29 days (one seed). The bound exists to cap on-chain traversal (D123).
4. **Layered support plus detection contains the cone.** Requiring every path intermediary to have D supported guarantors closer to the Council (D124) leaves 10 operators at 0.4% to 0.7% of accounts and about 0.01% of FCTY after 60 days (3 seeds on the retired simulator, 20 on the Rust one), against the cone strategy, an honest-first adaptive attacker and a supported-first attacker (to 120 days on 3 seeds, one year on 12). Cost to honest growth: about 3 days to reach 1000 accounts.
5. **Detection false positives are the main honest cost.** At a 1% false-positive rate with each partner inspected 10% of days, about 1000 genuine accounts are invalidated per year and the honest network stalls near 2 to 3 thousand; in 60-day sweeps honest growth recovers from 1888 to 2250-2400 accounts when the false-positive rate falls to 0.3%-0.1%, and the cone stays contained (year-long runs at the lower rates are pending in BACKLOG). Detection must keep spurious cases rare.
6. **Excluding disputed accounts from paths (D131) costs honest growth in proportion to the spurious-case rate.** After 60 days with inspection at 10% of partners per day: 42% fewer honest accounts at a 1% false-positive rate, 11% fewer at 0.3%, 2% fewer at 0.1%. Containment is unchanged (0.5% of accounts either way). The rule is cheap only with a precise detector.
7. **Caution toward strangers helps only if the look is informative.** With the plain 7-node bound the cone share falls from 29% to 9% when a stranger look has sensitivity 0.8 and false-positive rate 0.01, but only to 23% at 0.3 and 0.05; poor looks cost 7 to 14 days of honest growth. Layered support already contains the cone, so caution is a secondary lever.
8. **Operators are people, not a cone.** 100 operators over a year hold about 3% of accounts and of FCTY under layered support; they add no fake multiplier.

9. **Under the baseline the cone never forms, even with many or patient operators.** One year: 10, 30 and 100 operators hold 0.09%, 0.29% and 0.95% of accounts and at most 1.5% of FCTY; patient (120 days) and honest-first operators do no better; two years with 30 operators the same (diagnostic, 3 to 20 seeds). The attacker share is the operators divided by the honest accounts: the defense turns the sybil multiplier into one account per operator. Without layered support the same scenario leaves 31.7% of accounts at 60 days.
10. **Honest growth stalls near 10,000 active accounts (open, a design question; the false-positive load is a main cause, see the end of this entry).** When the difficulty D (digits of the active count) reaches 5, invitations need five disjoint paths of at most seven nodes to five different seats of an 11-seat Council; the Council-path gate then dominates the refusals and growth stops at about 10,100 accounts. Not an attacker effect. Levers compared at 500 days (4 seeds, 10 operators): baseline 10,482 honest accounts; path bound 9 instead of 7: 12,018; difficulty capped at 4: 12,422; owned-slot ceiling 300: 10,673; 21 Council seats from 100 active accounts: 17,715. The attackers stayed at 10 accounts and at most 0.1% of FCTY in all of them. The plateau is set mainly by the Council size against D (an 11-seat Council cannot supply five disjoint short paths), and the slot ceiling is not the cause (diagnostic). Council size against 100 operators over 500 days: 11 seats 10,324 honest accounts (attacker share 0.95%, dirty FCTY 0.92%), 21 seats 16,724 (0.60%, 0.59%), 31 seats 33,134 (0.30%, 0.43%), 51 seats 66,592 (0.15%, 0.31%): more seats grow the network several times larger and lower the attacker share at once, at the price of computation; a rule of seats at least a multiple of D gave 11,082 (3 per unit), 16,951 (4) and 24,917 (5, with attacker share 0.40% and dirty FCTY 0.55%): 6 per unit gave 32,491 and 8 per unit 49,887 (attacker share 0.31% and 0.20%); two years with 5 per unit gave 23,843, so each fixed rule has a plateau of its own, roughly 1,000 honest accounts per seat at D = 5. A Council that grows with D is a candidate rule; to remove the plateau at every power of ten the multiple has to grow with the network, which costs computation. The levers are candidates for network referendums (D144). Correction (diagnostic, 4 seeds, one year, 10 operators): the plateau is not set by the Council size alone. With the false-positive rate at 0 the honest network reaches 48,346 accounts, at 0.0003 19,619, at 0.001 11,292, at the baseline 0.003 10,373 and at 0.01 4,905; letting paths cross disputed accounts gives 35,294 at the baseline rate. Cases on genuine people lock accounts and, through the exclusion of disputed accounts from paths (D131), take intermediaries from the Council-path gate. One independent confirming look before a case opens removes almost all of those cases (2,950 to 16) and gives 38,496-39,244 accounts at the same attacker share; confirmation by the target's own partners starves in a fake cluster (attacker share 67.5% against 29.3% without layered support), confirmation by independent examiners does not (36.8%). Excluding only the accused party from paths (`--targets-only`) gives 31,068. Study: detection precision against growth (D153). Follow-up: BACKLOG, owner question Q20.

11. **The baseline verdict holds in absolute terms (diagnostic).** One year, 10 to 100 operators: attacker-side accounts never exceed the operators (9.7, 29.3, 98.2), an operator mints about 250 to 280 units a year (one Self-ID a day, as a participant does) and holds 130 to 160 of them, and the honest network is 0.98 to 1.00 of the attack-free control on the same seeds. The low shares are not a denominator effect. Study: [core closure](../studies/2026-11-06-core-closure/README.md#k1--the-metrics-measure-the-claim).
12. **Containment does not depend on the defense assumptions; the honest network does (diagnostic).** With 30 operators the dirty-FCTY share stays at most 0.67% across correlated examiner errors, a lower proof success, slower proof and less attention. The honest network is most sensitive to the time an accused person needs to prove reality (0.83 of the control at 14 days, 0.56 at 28), then to correlated errors (0.71 at 5% odd profiles with 30 times the rate); the proof success moves the number of wrongly invalidated people (16 to 138), not the network; less attention improves the network (inspection 0.003: 1.32 of the control) because the inspection itself is a cost through false cases. The mechanism is the dispute hold and the exclusion of disputed accounts from paths (D131): the Q20 variants remove the damage of a slow proof. Study: [K2](../studies/2026-11-06-core-closure/README.md#k2--hidden-assumptions-of-the-defense).
13. **False accusations do not starve the network but wrongly invalidate people in proportion (diagnostic).** Operators filing up to three false cases a day each reach the honest network at 1.03 to 1.05 of the control because the XP gate keeps accusers away from high-XP intermediaries and an accuser is held by its own case (its emission falls from 280 to a few units a year); about 0.5% of false cases end in a wrongly invalidated person (158 people a year at the heaviest setting). Whether the accuser should pay is a normative question (Q21). Study: [K3](../studies/2026-11-06-core-closure/README.md#k3--false-accusations-as-an-attack-on-the-availability-of-the-defense).
14. **The honest network recovers from an error burst without an extra mechanism (diagnostic).** A thirty-day burst with ten times the false-positive rate takes the network to a trough of 0.69 of the control about two weeks after the burst ends; it returns to 90% of the control 40 to 170 days after the burst ends (five of six seeds without remedies, six of six with appeal and link restoration, whose effect on the speed is inside the seed spread). Appeal and restoration (D151) are therefore not shown to be required for recovery. Study: [K4](../studies/2026-11-06-core-closure/README.md#k4--recovery-after-a-burst-of-errors).
15. **The frozen strongest adversary does not break the criterion on reserved seeds (confirmation in the stated bounds, diagnostic in the sense of AGENTS).** 100 operators, supported-first, patience 120 days, 20% bought honest accounts: 12 of 12 reserved seeds pass both D129 lines at one year (dirty-FCTY share at most 1.66%, attacker share at most 0.93%), as does the 100-operator reference (12 of 12, at most 1.49%); two years: 12 of 12 (dirty-FCTY share at most 1.54%, attacker share at most 0.89%). Study: [K5](../studies/2026-11-06-core-closure/README.md#k5--break-attempts-and-reserved-seed-confirmation).

**Core readiness (D155, recommendation pending the owner; K1 and K5 re-checked under the emission path check D156 and again under the softer D131 of D157: 36 of 36 reserved-seed runs pass on the new family 2001-2012, dirty-FCTY share at most 1.66%; the other numbers below were measured under the earlier readings).** *Core to implement:* the mechanics baseline of the [simulator contract](simulator.en.md): the mutual-guarantee graph with owned slots and valences (D81-D107), invitations that need D internally vertex-disjoint current paths of at most seven nodes to D different Council members with layered support for intermediaries (D104, D123, D124, D134), the Council lookup (D33, D133), the 24-hour freeze veto, formal cases through one procedure with the XP gate and a seven-day window (§25, D77, D131), proof of reality outside the network (D136) and detection by partners, public evidence and relay. *Needed by ablation or measurement:* layered support (without it the cone takes 29%-32% of accounts, with it 0.6%-1%); detection stays on in every run. *Not needed for containment:* the exclusion of the accused from paths (D131 as softened by D157; the earlier exclusion of both parties cost two thirds of the honest network), appeal and link restoration (D151; no measurable effect on recovery), the subtree burden (neutral under layered support), K by XP level (no effect), a larger Council (fixed by D33). *Supported in the stated bounds:* the D129 lines hold against 10 to 100 operators, the frozen strongest adversary and every one-factor weakening tested, with 36 of 36 reserved-seed runs passing at one and two years; the honest network is within seed spread of the attack-free control; the network recovers from error bursts; false accusations do not starve it. *Breaks or degrades:* without layered support; a four-week proof window (0.56 of the control); strongly correlated examiner errors (0.71); wrongly invalidated people grow linearly with false cases; the honest network sat near 10,000 accounts under the Council rule and the false-case load and sits near 34,000 at one year under D157; a four-week proof window still leaves about half of the network (0.51), which the shortening window of D158 would address. *Not claimed:* profitability or the price of attacks, any behavior of real people, anything about a network that is not human-operated. *Decision (recommendation):* proceed to implement the base protocol within these bounds, with Q20 (verification before a case, softer D131) and Q21 (a bound on the proof window, a tolerance for honest damage, the cost of a false accusation) settled in the implementation specification; no selected rule is changed. The research loop stops here; further work needs an owner request. Study: [core closure](../studies/2026-11-06-core-closure/README.md).

16. **The verdict survives other honest connectivity regimes (diagnostic).** Sparse, dense, stranger-friendly and stranger-averse honest behavior with 30 and 100 operators for a year: the D129 lines hold in all twelve configurations (dirty-FCTY share at most 2.33%, in the sparse regime with 100 operators), attacker-side accounts never exceed the operators, and the honest network is 0.92 to 1.02 of its paired control. Sparse connectivity is the weakest regime. Study: [honest regimes](../studies/2026-11-06-core-closure/README.md#honest-behavior-regimes-backlog-item-diagnostic).

17. **Social recovery (D135) works but is slow and incomplete in the scenario (diagnostic).** At key-loss rates of 5% to 50% a year, about two thirds of the recoveries that start finish, in a month on average, and the D129 lines are unaffected; only 40% to 49% of lost accounts are recovered within the year, and neither a higher slot ceiling nor certain vouching moves the funnel (the new account paying one right per guarantee is a plausible but unverified cause). With 20% to 30% bought accounts 0.5% to 4% of lost accounts could be captured through recovery (counted, not executed). Study: [key loss and recovery](../studies/2026-11-06-core-closure/README.md#key-loss-and-social-recovery-d135-scenario-diagnostic).

18. **Excluding only the accused from paths triples the honest network at the same containment (D157, diagnostic).** One year, 4 seeds: honest accounts with 30 operators 11,330 to 31,886, honest emission 0.86 to 2.82 million units, dirty-FCTY share 0.20% to 0.18% (30 operators) and 0.77% to 0.62% (100), the cone without layered support 27.0% to 24.9%; the reserved-seed confirmation of the re-frozen adversary passes 36 of 36 runs. A fixed dispute window of 3, 5, 7 and 14 days gives 41,321, 34,863, 31,886 and 26,306 honest accounts. Study: [D157 re-check](../studies/2026-11-06-core-closure/README.md#re-check-under-the-softer-d131-d157).

19. **Crediting XP at maturity instead of at spending changes nothing that matters for containment, and the retroactive-XP exploit is closed by construction (maturity: diagnostic, exploit: tested).** One year, 4 seeds: honest accounts within seed noise of the spend-credit baseline (control 38,970 against 33,610, per-seed spread 31 thousand to 50 thousand), dirty-FCTY share 0.17% and 0.55% with 30 and 100 operators against 0.18% and 0.62%. Hoarders earn XP without spending, so Council rankings differ and the prefix to 1000 accounts takes about 10 days longer. The relation that becomes active after a Self-ID never earns XP from it, by named regression tests for an invitation and for a valence that turns mutual later. (D163, [study](../studies/2026-11-07-maturity-and-ladder/README.md))
20. **The XP halving of losing participants, applied to examiners who file on noisy looks, collapses honest growth to about a third (confirmed on reserved seeds, conditional; the channel is only partly identified).** Attack-free control, seeds 3001-3004, one year: 12.4 to 12.8 thousand honest accounts with the full ladder against 34 to 50 thousand without the halving (ratio 2.7 to 4.0 on every seed); stakes, custody and joining behavior are not the cause (ablations within 1%). Sparing Council members from the halving recovers 43% to 62% of the no-halving count and cuts Council turnover by two thirds, so Council churn is a channel but not the whole explanation (the registered claim that it is the channel was killed on seed 3004). One independent confirming look before a case opens (unselected, D153) removes the loss (1.13 to 1.48 times the no-halving count, about 25 cases a year). Follow-up: Q24 in [DECISIONS_NEEDED](../DECISIONS_NEEDED.md) and [BACKLOG](../BACKLOG.md). ([study](../studies/2026-11-07-maturity-and-ladder/README.md), D159)
21. **Stakes make false accusation a cost and leave containment intact (exploratory; containment confirmed on reserved seeds).** Operators who hold no liquid FCTY cannot open any false case (10,640 refused first stakes a year with 30 operators); operators who convert every emission open about 400 a year, lose 7 FCTY and half their XP each time the accused proves reality, and leave the honest network unchanged (12,390 against 12,450 accounts), where the abstraction without stakes allowed 10,430 accusations a year and 355 wrongly invalidated people. The frozen strongest adversary stays at a dirty-FCTY share of 0.81% to 0.89% and an attacker share of about 0.2% on 4 of 4 reserved seeds under the full ladder. (D159, D129)
22. **The three-day window floor is not a duration bound; Council attention is (diagnostic, closed form plus engine test).** An adversary with a chain of eligible accounts that enters in the last hour before each deadline holds an accused person for 21.9 days with 3 entries, 42.6 with 10, 72.2 with 20 (last stake 269 FCTY, 1,587 FCTY locked); the Council's terminal entry ends the escalation, so with a Council member looking at a contested dispute with probability 0.3 per day the mean is 14 days whatever the chain length. The bound rests on a Council-attention assumption that is not calibrated. (D162, D161)
23. **The ladder's guarantor charge scales with guarantor counts, not with the stake.** The base stake of 7 FCTY is charged to each guarantor of a losing accused person; accounts have dozens of mutual guarantors (Council members 70 to 90), so one wrongful invalidation burns on the order of 200 FCTY of honest guarantors, 17,641 FCTY for 88 wrongful invalidations in one seed-year of the control. This is the rule as selected (D159, diagnostic); whether that magnitude is intended is not asked beyond Q24.

24. **After a burst of false positives the honest network recovers under the ladder, deeper and slower than under the abstract disputes (exploratory).** Thirty days at ten times the false-positive rate, four seeds: trough 0.45 to 0.72 of the paired control, back to 90% 77 to 222 days after the burst ends (4 of 4 within the year); with one confirming look before a case opens the burst leaves no trace. ([study](../studies/2026-11-07-maturity-and-ladder/README.md), D141)

25. **The stress panel under the ladder keeps both D129 lines in all nine variants and shows the honest cost drivers (exploratory).** 30 operators, one year, 4 seeds: attacker share at most 0.31%, dirty-FCTY share at most 0.29%; honest accounts 12.1 to 13.4 thousand across joining rates, Council attention, proof weakening, odd-profile examiners, cash-out and key loss; sparse and dense honest regimes and Council attention up to 1.0 per day keep both lines too (honest network 0.95 to 1.02 of the paired control); a slower proof of reality is the largest cost (63 to 398 genuine invalidations, 9.9 FCTY lost per honest account-year); the ladder halves the top of the XP distribution and triples Council turnover. Corrupt examiners are modeled as absent, which is why the bought-account variant grows to 45.6 thousand. ([study](../studies/2026-11-07-maturity-and-ladder/README.md))

26. **With examiners who check twice before filing, the selected ladder and halving cost honest growth nothing and containment holds (registered on reserved seeds, conditional on examiner behavior).** Seeds 4001-4004, one year: honest accounts 3.0 to 4.8 times those of single-look examiners (36 to 58 thousand), no genuine person invalidated in the control against 56 to 93, formal cases about 13 a year, and the frozen strongest adversary at a dirty-FCTY share of 0.77% to 1.11% and an attacker share of at most 0.27%. This answers finding 20 as the owner (D164) read it: the loss came from examiners filing on one noisy look; the halving rule is unchanged. Unverified: real examiner behavior. ([study](../studies/2026-11-07-maturity-and-ladder/README.md))

27. **XP as units of responsibility times an integrity coefficient (D165) keeps containment and makes a penalty recoverable (registered on reserved seeds, conditional on examiner behavior).** Seeds 5001-5004: the frozen strongest adversary stays at a dirty-FCTY share of 0.80% to 1.02% and an attacker share of at most 0.23%; with single-look examiners 84% to 90% of the roughly 1,500 accounts that lost integrity have recovered part of it within a year (1/1000 per own Self-ID), yet honest growth stays near 12 thousand accounts, so the slow restoration does not rescue careless examiners; with two-look examiners growth is 21 to 63 thousand accounts (1.8 to 5.1 times the single-look control; the claim of at least 2 times on every seed missed on one seed at 1.77 and was not killed). ([study](../studies/2026-11-07-maturity-and-ladder/README.md), D165)

28. **With soft stake backing false accusations are limited by the XP gate and integrity, not by funds, and the base stake changes the attackers' debt but not the number of cases (exploratory).** 30 operators accusing daily with half their emissions cashed out, seeds 1-4, one year: 3,741 to 3,758 cases opened against about 400 under liquid-only backing, attacker debt 25 thousand, 51 thousand and 102 thousand FCTY at base stakes 7, 14 and 28 (the debt is repaid by burning their emission), honest network 87% to 99% of the control, 11 genuine people invalidated a year. A debt ceiling or a minimum backing is an open design point. (D166, [study](../studies/2026-11-07-maturity-and-ladder/README.md))

29. **The formed baseline (D162-D167) passes the registered containment and growth lines on reserved seeds, except one false-accusation seed (registered, conditional on the examiner and joining assumptions).** Seeds 6001-6004: the frozen strongest adversary at a dirty-FCTY share of 0.75% to 1.28% and an attacker share of at most 0.32%; control honest accounts 3.6 to 4.8 times those of single-look examiners with 1 genuine person invalidated a year against 54 to 70; under daily false accusations the honest network is 0.78 to 1.18 of the control, so the registered 80% line was crossed on one seed (6001, 0.78) and that claim was killed. ([study](../studies/2026-11-07-maturity-and-ladder/README.md))

Retired directions (recorded once, not to be revisited without new evidence): attack simulations without detection (item 1); per-pair invitation rights as a defense (D120, D121: a delay of a few days, not carried into the Rust simulator); a capacity-consuming path variant (not selected, in tension with D35).

30. **Conditional containment does not supply independent evidence or finite corrective service (D168, diagnostic).** Corrected mechanics `e9b0515`, identical-core invoker `0b90e6e`: all 18 local and 20 final population runs finish, the four required final arms each on reserved seeds 8001-8004. Every final endpoint passes D129 (worst dirty 0.040191%, minimum clean-account fraction 93.9752%), with no false accounts accepted. Default-attack honest issuance is 0.552-0.897 of matched control; joint/default-control issuance is 0.011-0.077, with headcount effects varying by seed and severe environment damage even without attackers. Controlled repeated looks show that rereading the same evidence loses independent-error reduction; human joint errors remain unmeasured. A zero-backed losing accuser pays a winning defender zero and abandonment leaves debt unpaid; a continuing mistaken examiner clears seven debt units by day 14 but restores its coefficient only on day 507. A qualified 21-position late ladder lasts 72 days 4 hours with no collected coalition forfeits under zero backing (42 preparation FCTY remain real costs); a one-case Council queue leaves two of three cases unserved and one innocent accused losing. Recovery of a wrongly invalidated accused requires an explicitly admitted appeal. These are conditional transition witnesses, not human incidence estimates. The block is the unverified premise that independent information and willing timely defense/appeal will be available and financed by nominal stakes. Actual providers, finite workload, evidence access and voluntary incentives need an explicit next-stage decision (Q25), not a new selected rule or more synthetic matrices. No profitable-honesty, universal resistance or debt-bound claim follows. Source: [final report, mechanism table and native evidence](../studies/2026-11-08-evidence-core/README.md). No follow-up is active; parked work belongs to BACKLOG.

## Design discussion reconciliation (2026-10-23)

**Source:** the protocol owner's A–T discussion synthesis supplied in the research/development mandate. These are documentation dates, not fabricated historical decision dates. Matching D entries above are reused. Owner-stated intentions are DIRECTION or restatements of SELECTED rules; behavioral predictions are HYPOTHESIS. Reviewer cautions, proposed controls and code suspicions are research interpretations, not owner-approved transitions. Every prediction below remains unconfirmed unless a separately cited study says otherwise. [Causal loops and hypothesis matrix](research.en.md) supply study designs; [Checkpoint 1](https://github.com/llblab/facety/blob/legacy-node/BACKLOG.md#checkpoint-1-accountable-correction-mandate) owns remaining work.

- **A — XP pays responsibility (D2, D17; §17.5, §23).** Owner rationale: guarantees create work and exposure worth rewarding. HYPOTHESIS: high-XP builders are better informed, committed and long-term-oriented; not a simulator definition. The historical D2 terminal subject/guarantor credits are implemented/tested in `test/model.test.mjs`; D54 now implements ordinary opt-in D42 maturation; full default/frozen/debt migration remains gated. Falsifier: equal-evidence high-XP actors are less responsive or less accurate than newcomers. Follow-up: alignment/authority controls, not removal of XP merely for inequality.
- **B — Earned early advantage (D16; §13, §23).** DIRECTION favors early builders; SELECTED non-transferability prevents direct token purchase of XP, not bribery, coercion, controlled keys, delegated influence or coalitions of real people. Bootstrap/XP exist; no permanent honesty is encoded in the model. Falsifier: early status prevents correction of established misconduct or entry becomes inaccessible. Follow-up: long-horizon mobility, indirect influence and apex-case tests.
- **C — Attributable public histories (D15; §9, §39).** DIRECTION makes signed responsibility inspectable. Research interpretation: publication, observation, comprehension, escalation, adjudication and enforcement are different stages. Public model events and noisy inspection exist; universal attention does not. Falsifier: public violations repeatedly fail before usable review despite available evidence. Follow-up: stage clocks, attention/outage and evidence-access controls.
- **D — Reputation differs from XP (D22; §4, §23, §26.5).** Distinguish identity credibility, guarantee reliability, adjudication history, social recognition and protocol XP. High XP can accompany bad judgment; a newcomer can hold decisive evidence. The dispute-record prior exists, but wins are not independent truth and correlation/manipulation remain possible. Falsifier: a reputation proxy rejects stronger newcomer evidence or reinforces correlated errors. Follow-up: source/status-matched review and prior ablations.
- **E — Network credibility and future stakes (D16; §9, §27.4).** Owner HYPOTHESIS: builders benefit from useful future relationships/services and continued network credibility. Sunk work is not automatically future loss; misconduct need not always destroy value, and sincere insiders may think concealment protects the network. Current valuations are finite-horizon inputs, not an endogenous credibility model. Falsifier: unilateral concealment remains preferable across plausible future stakes. Follow-up: explicit opportunity streams, discounting, beliefs and individual deviations.
- **F — Bottom-up withdrawal (D9; §19.3).** DIRECTION: losing relationships/XP can constrain influential actors without Council permission. `endGuarantee` revokes the guarantor's historical credits when either party ends the edge, charges the ending party one liquid FCTY into fee custody, and suppresses later snapshot credits while no edge exists; historical vetoes can persist. Liability is separate: this reference's exit escape is historical/pre-D34 behavior, not the new past-event responsibility rule; D34 migration and re-link/event-order semantics remain unimplemented. Prediction/falsifier: the same expected XP loss may improve initial selection but deter later exit. Follow-up: both-party, pending-act, re-link and concealment tests with matched watcher capacity.
- **G — Multiple human correction routes (D8, D29; §20.5).** Intended routes are self-correction, guarantor intervention/exit, evidence-backed challenge, Chekharda and Council—not an infallible algorithm. Appropriate early reporting is favored, but exact immunity limits remain OPEN. Veto/exit/dispute abstractions exist; self-report eligibility does not. Falsifier: correction is inaccessible or a report-and-repeat cycle is profitable. D34 now specifies the intended event-level money/XP reversal and historical liability basis, but not its eligibility/deadline/funding contract. Follow-up: bounded early-correction reference policies after those definitions.
- **H — Collective final authority (D3; §27).** At the A–T checkpoint the SELECTED Council target was 101 active eligible high-XP subjects; D33 now replaces fixed size prospectively while retaining **one equal vote each**, not an XP-weighted president. A consul can challenge another through ordinary/collective routes; seniority is not immunity. Active eligibility is implemented; merits/referral/quorum remain OPEN. Falsifier: apex cases cannot reach a responsive collective or conflicted votes block correction. Follow-up: consul-versus-consul reference procedures; no invented higher sovereign.
- **I — Recalculation is not turnover (D3; §27.1).** Research interpretation: dynamic rankings can be stable; neither stability nor frequent replacement is a health verdict. Member IDs/churn are measured, while contestability and independence are not established. Falsifier: established misconduct cannot remove influence despite nominal recalculation. Follow-up: actual mobility, tie boundaries, judgment correlation and influence-removal tests.
- **J — A motivated minority (D11; §40.1).** Owner HYPOTHESIS: a small investigative population plus responsive decision-makers can serve many passive people. Discovery and authorization are different roles. Daily inspection budgets exist; vacations, missed notices, shared blind spots and review queues are incomplete. Falsifier: more investigators only increase backlog or passive participation collapses under load. Follow-up: matched-resource capacity/notification sweeps.
- **K — Discovery beyond membership (D28; §25.1).** DIRECTION: neither membership nor XP is needed to notice an inconsistency or construct useful evidence; formal sponsorship has separate eligibility/responsibility. External observers must not gain invented membership, XP, private access or procedural rights. Explicit relay was absent at the baseline. Falsifier: strong outsider/low-XP evidence has no reachable willing sponsor at comparable resources. Follow-up: the evidence-to-outcome vertical slice and apex limitation.
- **L — Optional private compensation (D28; §25.1).** DIRECTION permits investigator/sponsor agreements outside protocol, not a mandatory bounty market, new token or transferable XP. Research assumption must name payer, recipient, currency, source funding, work and payment/collection risk. No external FCTY mint is permitted. Falsifier: net costs or nonpayment prevent participation. Follow-up: funded conditional/off-protocol compensation arms, separate from unpaid routing.
- **M — Exposure beyond local collusion (D28; §25.1, §40.1).** HYPOTHESIS: outsiders, rivals and former collaborators can route evidence around an accused/guarantor cluster. Access, sponsor conflict, capacity and dishonesty can defeat it; multiple sponsors/independent routes/publication are candidate controls, not omnipotent fallbacks. Falsifier: colluding local actors still block all usable routes under declared access/resources. Follow-up: rerouting, sponsor loss and correlated bottleneck tests.
- **N — Source allegiance is not evidence correctness (§21, §25.1).** Research boundary: malicious true reports may help; respected false reports may harm. An allegation is not guilt, nor public evidence an infallible flag. Environment signals remain fallible. The bounded relay now supplies source-independent review and forged-ID/schema/duplicate controls, not authenticated evidence or error independence. Falsifier: identical evidence is accepted/rejected solely by evaluator allegiance or one circulated source becomes many confirmations. Follow-up: truth-label isolation and provenance tests.
- **O — Dense honest communities and scalable fake exposure (D17; §23, §40.1).** Genuine dense early groups are intended, not automatically attacks. Owner HYPOTHESIS: larger fake farms struggle with fresh media, acquaintances, real-encounter claims and public flows. Correlated detection, camouflage, economies of fabrication and finite capacity can reverse scaling. `fakeSelfId`, screening and flow cues exist, not guaranteed scale-dependent detection. Falsifier: matched genuine groups are punished or camouflage/scale reduce exposure. Follow-up: genuine/collusive/false controls with equal size, age, resources and topology.
- **P — Network effects must have mechanisms (D24; §9, §34).** HYPOTHESIS: propagation, cooperation and repeated participation can be nonlinear; no unexplained quadratic honesty bonus is authorized. Gossip is implemented. Arithmetic XP multiplication, population growth, useful services and manipulation resistance are separate quantities; both sides can use gossip/rewards. Falsifier: an effect survives only as encoded arithmetic or an imposed script. Follow-up: presence/necessity/alternative controls and useful-service calibration.
- **Q — Bounded fraud and amplified authority (D27; §9).** DIRECTION allows explicitly bounded harm, not a promise that any small money share is safe. Money/XP shares, gates, seats, wrongful decisions, obstruction and trends need separate reporting. Existing seat metrics do not show corrupt merits. Falsifier: a small XP contamination crosses a decisive gate or harm grows after neglect. Follow-up: near-threshold authority and survivability-vector studies.
- **R — Self-protecting abuse (§27.4, §41.9).** Central research question: do accumulated violations buy the ability to prevent later correction, or does visible abuse attract enough investigation/sponsorship to reverse it? Current scripts/seat shares cannot decide. Falsifier of resilience: ordinary restored conditions fail to correct without resetting history or adding a ruler. Falsifier of capture: strong cases remain accessible and remove influence. Follow-up: registered recovery/obstruction criteria with both feedbacks active.
- **S — Reasoned precedent (D30; §27.5).** Owner proposal, not selected mechanics: public reasons can guide comparable ambiguous cases. Interpretation differs from prospective rule change; retroactive punishment and bad-precedent lock-in are risks. No learning/precedent engine exists. Falsifier: insider/newcomer inconsistency persists or a bad precedent cannot be corrected. Follow-up: status-matched reasoned/unexplained decisions with appeal/finality assumptions.
- **T — Corrected critique scope (all above).** Research correction: Facety has intended routes to challenge senior participants; accessibility/effectiveness remain open. Coalition seats are not demonstrated malicious governance, fixed loyalty is not individual rationality, and exploratory numbers are not real-world facts. The original five studies were reread for this audit; they remain conditional historical artifacts. Falsifier: any stronger claim lacks source-bound evidence. Follow-up: independently review claims and new confirmation registrations rather than repeating superseded critiques.

## Design-analysis insights

A2–A7 retain protocol-design arguments from the owner's review discussion on 2026-10-23; A8 adds dated source analysis and diagnostic software checks. None establishes a calibrated economic result, emergent mechanism or newly selected rule. Each names its discriminating test or validation and backlog owner. They extend and do not replace the analysis hypothesis below (A1).

**A2 — ANALYSIS, the two distribution layers are asymmetric.** FCTY emission is per-capita-flat: one eligible Self-ID creates one emission right for the subject, rate-limited to one rewarded act per day (§17.5). XP is explicitly not flat: a participant with many active guarantee relations earns far more than one XP per day, and "equal XP distribution is not a design goal" (§23). The owner's inverse-Bitcoin fairness argument — emission cannot be scaled by capital, unlike mined supply concentrated in early hands — therefore holds for money and does not hold for procedural authority, which is intentionally super-linear in social position. Distribution-fairness claims must state both layers. Owner nuance retained: the 1 FCTY burn is an invitation cost and not automatically every guarantee commitment (§19.2), and paying an entry cost is not the same as paying a risk premium. No test needed for the definition itself; the consequence is tested by the guarantee-selection game in [BACKLOG.md](../BACKLOG.md).

**A3 — ANALYSIS, flat compensation with an increasing tail needs a repricing mechanism.** Guarantor compensation is fixed per successful act (one XP each, §23), while exposure is chain-proportional and, under D34, attaches at event time regardless of a later exit (§21.1, §20.6). The only adjustment available is the link break, whose XP revocation (D9) makes it more expensive the more successful the relation has been. Influence growth therefore raises exposure and exit cost together, which is the structure of flat-premium insurance without repricing rather than evidence that the network cannot grow. The expected selection effect — preference for reciprocal and low-uncertainty guarantee pairs — is a hypothesis, not an observed outcome, and is consistent with the already retained cluster/coalition findings (F1, F10, F11, F21). Discriminating test recorded in the guarantee-selection game item; the owner's suspension proposal below (A4) is the cheapest candidate counter-mechanism.

**A4 — ANALYSIS, any liability-off mode needs a forward-only cutoff.** A suspension, vacation or visibility-off state that takes effect immediately converts into look-ahead escape: a guarantor who suspects a problem suspends before the damaging event and keeps network benefits while dropping duty and removing a watcher — the channel already recorded in F11, which the invitation freeze (F10) was selected to close. Therefore any such mode requires an activation delay that preserves liability for events occurring before it takes effect, must not be strictly better than an established-link break, and must state its effect on the verified count. Whether liability attaches to events during the notice period is a D34 contract choice, not a local parameter. Carried by the suspension-mode backlog item; no mode is selected.

**A5 — ANALYSIS, the media oracle's reliability is exogenous and the social layer's is endogenous.** The protocol already states that a photograph is not proof of unique personhood, and the owner's formulation extends this: the face reference is a fulcrum or anchor, not the proof itself. The village analogy supporting it holds where the population is closed and small, recognition is continuous and multimodal (gait, voice, context, history) and impersonation requires physical co-presence; Facety instead faces an open global population, single-shot artifacts and generative attackers, so the anchor supplies continuity of presentation over time rather than uniqueness, and its false-accept rate is set by oracle capability that improves adversarially. The falsifiable form: either protocol conclusions are insensitive to the declared face accuracy range — which supports the owner's claim that guarantees and history carry the budget — or they are sensitive, which makes the media layer load-bearing and requires a stated degradation path. Test recorded as the layer-sensitivity item in [BACKLOG.md](../BACKLOG.md).

**A6 — ANALYSIS, protection of a low-XP participant rests on un-funded attention.** The staged ladder ends with the last unopposed side (§26.5), so exhaustion — not only error — loses; and out-of-protocol sponsorship (§25.1) makes exposure profitable where a payer exists. The owner's counterargument that this is maximal distribution rather than exploitation is right about emission and does not answer the procedure: reputation during selection is an assumption, not a right. Candidate levers already inside the design vocabulary: pay the first challenger from the collected sanction pool instead of from the position above, credit sponsorship for unrepresented low-XP accusers, and cap removals at the ladder's early depths where F13 shows Council intervention restores accuracy. Any lever must be shown not to make frivolous challenges profitable (F6's harassment boundary).

**A7 — ANALYSIS, price stabilization sits exactly on a D32 falsifier.** The owner proposes Treasury D buyback as the mechanism that stabilizes price over time. D32 states the thesis fails if retention depends only on assumed token prices or subsidies rather than demonstrated usefulness. Retention supported by external buyback capital therefore belongs to the falsifier side until tested: compare retained participation with and without bucket-D support under declared valuations. Already covered by the Atmo calibration item's subsidy-dependence acceptance in [BACKLOG.md](../BACKLOG.md); recorded here so the tension is not forgotten, without opening a second mandate.

**A8 — ANALYSIS from source inspection, with diagnostic software checks, 2026-10-24: equal one-day maturities remove the legacy fixture's own-XP timing premium under an earliest-use comparison.** `src/incentives.mjs` pins 72/168-hour maturity, zero invitation freeze and immediate acceptance in its legacy scenario; `FacetyModel` currently requires 24-hour spend, conversion and invitation freeze by default. For the separately implemented [P23 counterpart](https://github.com/llblab/facety/blob/legacy-node/docs/incentives.en.md#one-day-counterpart-contract-p23), a sponsor act recorded at 0 h is terminally disposed at 24 h both by earliest invitation and earliest waiting conversion, so the old day-3 versus day-7 own-XP timing difference cannot be inherited. Admission then waits until 48 h; a fresh newborn act at 48 h can convert at 72 h. Equal-hour admission/work need journal order, not invented sub-hour clocks or a D34 legal-event choice. The pre-data reporting design therefore retains both snapshots and does not estimate work-at-admission by an `atHour <= 48` filter, which would wrongly include later same-hour identity work. This conditional API/timing deduction is now checked by the [eight one-day software regression families and exact legacy preservation controls](../studies/2026-10-24-one-day-incentives/README.md), not a new empirical/mechanism claim, calibrated payoff, actual preference or retention finding. F45 now retains freshly reconstructed diagnostic tables/thresholds without copying or reclassifying historical outputs; expected future losses remain unknown despite no scripted debt/verdicts. The bounded one-day port/report is complete; the existing guarantee-selection/alignment owners retain the stronger choice and liability questions. No new reward/liability rule or general attack/benefit result is selected.

## Comparative analysis: Facety and Bitcoin

| Aspect | Bitcoin | Facety |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What secures it | Physical cost: energy and hardware (proof of work) | Social cost: daily fresh presence, guarantees with liability, public scrutiny |
| Sybil resistance | One unit of hashpower, one vote; identity irrelevant | One person, one identity; identity is the point |
| Unit of value | Scarce coin (21M cap, halvings) | Emission per active person per day: supply grows with the population; burns and buyback counteract |
| Main asset | The coin itself | The trust graph and XP (who is a real, accountable person); FCTY is the distribution mechanism |
| Trust assumption | Honest majority of hashpower | Hypothesized honest Council majority and denser honest network; no established global person-fraction economic guarantee (D62) |
| Privacy | Pseudonymous | Deliberately public and accountable |
| What it enables | Neutral, censorship-resistant money and settlement | One-person-one-vote, Sybil-resistant distribution, reputation, human verification in a world of AI agents |

**Analysis hypothesis A1 (not a calibrated economic finding or owner-selected monetary rule):** higher FCTY value may increase fake-identity incentives while human attention/collection capacity need not scale automatically. The response of sanctions, investigator compensation, fabrication technology, useful services and Treasury D policy needs a separate conditional model. A guaranteed inverse price/security relation, guaranteed application value, and a policy that sanctions must scale with price do not follow. The Bitcoin analogy is explanatory, not empirical validation of Facety.

## Method lessons

- Random scripted runs cannot model incentives. Use strategy menus, payoff facts and equilibrium or deviation analysis ([games](games.en.md)).
- A strategy menu that is too small misleads: adding `farm-and-harass` reversed the apparent safety of inaccurate verdicts.
- Check valuation traps: debt left on abandoned fakes is not a cost to the attacker (`falseDebt: +1`).
- Exploratory candidate counts are not discoveries (228 candidates, two of them confirmed).
- Confirm on reserved seeds before calling anything a mechanism; record what was not tested.
