# Insight register

This register keeps every design insight and research finding in one place. A **decision** comes from the protocol owner and is recorded in the [protocol](facety.protocol.en.md) with its label. A **finding** comes from the simulator and states its evidence level:

- **Confirmed:** supported by a preregistered test on reserved seeds ([evidence](evidence.en.md)).
- **Exploratory:** a grid, screen or game on one seed batch. It is a map, not a result.
- **Diagnostic:** a small targeted probe that explains a mechanism.

All findings are conditional on the simulator's declared assumptions, horizons and valuations. None is a real-world measurement. Update this file whenever a decision is made or a study ends.

## Design discussion reconciliation (2026-10-23)

**Source:** the protocol owner's A–T discussion synthesis supplied in the research/development mandate. These are documentation dates, not fabricated historical decision dates. Matching D entries above are reused. Owner-stated intentions are DIRECTION or restatements of SELECTED rules; behavioral predictions are HYPOTHESIS. Reviewer cautions, proposed controls and code suspicions are research interpretations, not owner-approved transitions. Every prediction below remains unconfirmed unless a separately cited study says otherwise. [Causal loops and hypothesis matrix](research.en.md) supply study designs; [Checkpoint 1](../BACKLOG.md#checkpoint-1-accountable-correction-mandate) owns remaining work.

- **A — XP pays responsibility (D2, D17; §17.5, §23).** Owner rationale: guarantees create work and exposure worth rewarding. HYPOTHESIS: high-XP builders are better informed, committed and long-term-oriented; not a simulator definition. The historical D2 terminal subject/guarantor credits are implemented/tested in `test/model.test.mjs`; D54 now implements ordinary opt-in D42 maturation; full default/frozen/debt migration remains gated. Falsifier: equal-evidence high-XP actors are less responsive or less accurate than newcomers. Follow-up: alignment/authority controls, not removal of XP merely for inequality.
- **B — Earned early advantage (D16; §13, §23).** DIRECTION favors early builders; SELECTED non-transferability prevents direct token purchase of XP, not bribery, coercion, controlled keys, delegated influence or coalitions of real people. Bootstrap/XP exist; no permanent honesty is encoded in the model. Falsifier: early status prevents correction of established misconduct or entry becomes inaccessible. Follow-up: long-horizon mobility, indirect influence and apex-case tests.
- **C — Attributable public histories (D15; §9, §39).** DIRECTION makes signed responsibility inspectable. Research interpretation: publication, observation, comprehension, escalation, adjudication and enforcement are different stages. Public model events and noisy inspection exist; universal attention does not. Falsifier: public violations repeatedly fail before usable review despite available evidence. Follow-up: stage clocks, attention/outage and evidence-access controls.
- **D — Reputation differs from XP (D22; §4, §23, §26.5).** Distinguish identity credibility, guarantee reliability, adjudication history, social recognition and protocol XP. High XP can accompany bad judgment; a newcomer can hold decisive evidence. The dispute-record prior exists, but wins are not independent truth and correlation/manipulation remain possible. Falsifier: a reputation proxy rejects stronger newcomer evidence or reinforces correlated errors. Follow-up: source/status-matched review and prior ablations.
- **E — Network credibility and future stakes (D16; §9, §27.4).** Owner HYPOTHESIS: builders benefit from useful future relationships/services and continued network credibility. Sunk work is not automatically future loss; misconduct need not always destroy value, and sincere insiders may think concealment protects the network. Current valuations are finite-horizon inputs, not an endogenous credibility model. Falsifier: unilateral concealment remains preferable across plausible future stakes. Follow-up: explicit opportunity streams, discounting, beliefs and individual deviations.
- **F — Bottom-up withdrawal (D9; §19.3).** DIRECTION: losing relationships/XP can constrain influential actors without Council permission. `endGuarantee` revokes the guarantor's historical credits when either party ends the edge, charges the ending party one liquid FACY into fee custody, and suppresses later snapshot credits while no edge exists; historical vetoes can persist. Liability is separate: this reference's exit escape is historical/pre-D34 behavior, not the new past-event responsibility rule; D34 migration and re-link/event-order semantics remain unimplemented. Prediction/falsifier: the same expected XP loss may improve initial selection but deter later exit. Follow-up: both-party, pending-act, re-link and concealment tests with matched watcher capacity.
- **G — Multiple human correction routes (D8, D29; §20.5).** Intended routes are self-correction, guarantor intervention/exit, evidence-backed challenge, Chekharda and Council—not an infallible algorithm. Appropriate early reporting is favored, but exact immunity limits remain OPEN. Veto/exit/dispute abstractions exist; self-report eligibility does not. Falsifier: correction is inaccessible or a report-and-repeat cycle is profitable. D34 now specifies the intended event-level money/XP reversal and historical liability basis, but not its eligibility/deadline/funding contract. Follow-up: bounded early-correction reference policies after those definitions.
- **H — Collective final authority (D3; §27).** At the A–T checkpoint the SELECTED Council target was 101 active eligible high-XP subjects; D33 now replaces fixed size prospectively while retaining **one equal vote each**, not an XP-weighted president. A consul can challenge another through ordinary/collective routes; seniority is not immunity. Active eligibility is implemented; merits/referral/quorum remain OPEN. Falsifier: apex cases cannot reach a responsive collective or conflicted votes block correction. Follow-up: consul-versus-consul reference procedures; no invented higher sovereign.
- **I — Recalculation is not turnover (D3; §27.1).** Research interpretation: dynamic rankings can be stable; neither stability nor frequent replacement is a health verdict. Member IDs/churn are measured, while contestability and independence are not established. Falsifier: established misconduct cannot remove influence despite nominal recalculation. Follow-up: actual mobility, tie boundaries, judgment correlation and influence-removal tests.
- **J — A motivated minority (D11; §40.1).** Owner HYPOTHESIS: a small investigative population plus responsive decision-makers can serve many passive people. Discovery and authorization are different roles. Daily inspection budgets exist; vacations, missed notices, shared blind spots and review queues are incomplete. Falsifier: more investigators only increase backlog or passive participation collapses under load. Follow-up: matched-resource capacity/notification sweeps.
- **K — Discovery beyond membership (D28; §25.1).** DIRECTION: neither membership nor XP is needed to notice an inconsistency or construct useful evidence; formal sponsorship has separate eligibility/responsibility. External observers must not gain invented membership, XP, private access or procedural rights. Explicit relay was absent at the baseline. Falsifier: strong outsider/low-XP evidence has no reachable willing sponsor at comparable resources. Follow-up: the evidence-to-outcome vertical slice and apex limitation.
- **L — Optional private compensation (D28; §25.1).** DIRECTION permits investigator/sponsor agreements outside protocol, not a mandatory bounty market, new token or transferable XP. Research assumption must name payer, recipient, currency, source funding, work and payment/collection risk. No external FACY mint is permitted. Falsifier: net costs or nonpayment prevent participation. Follow-up: funded conditional/off-protocol compensation arms, separate from unpaid routing.
- **M — Exposure beyond local collusion (D28; §25.1, §40.1).** HYPOTHESIS: outsiders, rivals and former collaborators can route evidence around an accused/guarantor cluster. Access, sponsor conflict, capacity and dishonesty can defeat it; multiple sponsors/independent routes/publication are candidate controls, not omnipotent fallbacks. Falsifier: colluding local actors still block all usable routes under declared access/resources. Follow-up: rerouting, sponsor loss and correlated bottleneck tests.
- **N — Source allegiance is not evidence correctness (§21, §25.1).** Research boundary: malicious true reports may help; respected false reports may harm. An allegation is not guilt, nor public evidence an infallible flag. Environment signals remain fallible. The bounded relay now supplies source-independent review and forged-ID/schema/duplicate controls, not authenticated evidence or error independence. Falsifier: identical evidence is accepted/rejected solely by evaluator allegiance or one circulated source becomes many confirmations. Follow-up: truth-label isolation and provenance tests.
- **O — Dense honest communities and scalable fake exposure (D17; §23, §40.1).** Genuine dense early groups are intended, not automatically attacks. Owner HYPOTHESIS: larger fake farms struggle with fresh media, acquaintances, real-encounter claims and public flows. Correlated detection, camouflage, economies of fabrication and finite capacity can reverse scaling. `fakeSelfId`, screening and flow cues exist, not guaranteed scale-dependent detection. Falsifier: matched genuine groups are punished or camouflage/scale reduce exposure. Follow-up: genuine/collusive/false controls with equal size, age, resources and topology.
- **P — Network effects must have mechanisms (D24; §9, §34).** HYPOTHESIS: propagation, cooperation and repeated participation can be nonlinear; no unexplained quadratic honesty bonus is authorized. Gossip is implemented. Arithmetic XP multiplication, population growth, useful services and manipulation resistance are separate quantities; both sides can use gossip/rewards. Falsifier: an effect survives only as encoded arithmetic or an imposed script. Follow-up: presence/necessity/alternative controls and useful-service calibration.
- **Q — Bounded fraud and amplified authority (D27; §9).** DIRECTION allows explicitly bounded harm, not a promise that any small money share is safe. Money/XP shares, gates, seats, wrongful decisions, obstruction and trends need separate reporting. Existing seat metrics do not show corrupt merits. Falsifier: a small XP contamination crosses a decisive gate or harm grows after neglect. Follow-up: near-threshold authority and survivability-vector studies.
- **R — Self-protecting abuse (§27.4, §41.9).** Central research question: do accumulated violations buy the ability to prevent later correction, or does visible abuse attract enough investigation/sponsorship to reverse it? Current scripts/seat shares cannot decide. Falsifier of resilience: ordinary restored conditions fail to correct without resetting history or adding a ruler. Falsifier of capture: strong cases remain accessible and remove influence. Follow-up: registered recovery/obstruction criteria with both feedbacks active.
- **S — Reasoned precedent (D30; §27.5).** Owner proposal, not selected mechanics: public reasons can guide comparable ambiguous cases. Interpretation differs from prospective rule change; retroactive punishment and bad-precedent lock-in are risks. No learning/precedent engine exists. Falsifier: insider/newcomer inconsistency persists or a bad precedent cannot be corrected. Follow-up: status-matched reasoned/unexplained decisions with appeal/finality assumptions.
- **T — Corrected critique scope (all above).** Research correction: Facety has intended routes to challenge senior participants; accessibility/effectiveness remain open. Coalition seats are not demonstrated malicious governance, fixed loyalty is not individual rationality, and exploratory numbers are not real-world facts. The original five studies were reread for this audit; they remain conditional historical artifacts. Falsifier: any stronger claim lacks source-bound evidence. Follow-up: independently review claims and new confirmation registrations rather than repeating superseded critiques.

## Design-analysis insights

A2–A7 retain protocol-design arguments from the owner's review discussion on 2026-10-23; A8 adds dated source analysis and diagnostic software checks. None establishes a calibrated economic result, emergent mechanism or newly selected rule. Each names its discriminating test or validation and backlog owner. They extend and do not replace the analysis hypothesis below (A1).

**A2 — ANALYSIS, the two distribution layers are asymmetric.** FACY emission is per-capita-flat: one eligible Self-ID creates one emission right for the subject, rate-limited to one rewarded act per day (§17.5). XP is explicitly not flat: a participant with many active guarantee relations earns far more than one XP per day, and "equal XP distribution is not a design goal" (§23). The owner's inverse-Bitcoin fairness argument — emission cannot be scaled by capital, unlike mined supply concentrated in early hands — therefore holds for money and does not hold for procedural authority, which is intentionally super-linear in social position. Distribution-fairness claims must state both layers. Owner nuance retained: the 1 FACY burn is an invitation cost and not automatically every guarantee commitment (§19.2), and paying an entry cost is not the same as paying a risk premium. No test needed for the definition itself; the consequence is tested by the guarantee-selection game in [BACKLOG.md](../BACKLOG.md).

**A3 — ANALYSIS, flat compensation with an increasing tail needs a repricing mechanism.** Guarantor compensation is fixed per successful act (one XP each, §23), while exposure is chain-proportional and, under D34, attaches at event time regardless of a later exit (§21.1, §20.6). The only adjustment available is the link break, whose XP revocation (D9) makes it more expensive the more successful the relation has been. Influence growth therefore raises exposure and exit cost together, which is the structure of flat-premium insurance without repricing rather than evidence that the network cannot grow. The expected selection effect — preference for reciprocal and low-uncertainty guarantee pairs — is a hypothesis, not an observed outcome, and is consistent with the already retained cluster/coalition findings (F1, F10, F11, F21). Discriminating test recorded in the guarantee-selection game item; the owner's suspension proposal below (A4) is the cheapest candidate counter-mechanism.

**A4 — ANALYSIS, any liability-off mode needs a forward-only cutoff.** A suspension, vacation or visibility-off state that takes effect immediately converts into look-ahead escape: a guarantor who suspects a problem suspends before the damaging event and keeps network benefits while dropping duty and removing a watcher — the channel already recorded in F11, which the invitation freeze (F10) was selected to close. Therefore any such mode requires an activation delay that preserves liability for events occurring before it takes effect, must not be strictly better than an established-link break, and must state its effect on the verified count. Whether liability attaches to events during the notice period is a D34 contract choice, not a local parameter. Carried by the suspension-mode backlog item; no mode is selected.

**A5 — ANALYSIS, the media oracle's reliability is exogenous and the social layer's is endogenous.** The protocol already states that a photograph is not proof of unique personhood, and the owner's formulation extends this: the face reference is a fulcrum or anchor, not the proof itself. The village analogy supporting it holds where the population is closed and small, recognition is continuous and multimodal (gait, voice, context, history) and impersonation requires physical co-presence; Facety instead faces an open global population, single-shot artifacts and generative attackers, so the anchor supplies continuity of presentation over time rather than uniqueness, and its false-accept rate is set by oracle capability that improves adversarially. The falsifiable form: either protocol conclusions are insensitive to the declared face accuracy range — which supports the owner's claim that guarantees and history carry the budget — or they are sensitive, which makes the media layer load-bearing and requires a stated degradation path. Test recorded as the layer-sensitivity item in [BACKLOG.md](../BACKLOG.md).

**A6 — ANALYSIS, protection of a low-XP participant rests on un-funded attention.** The staged ladder ends with the last unopposed side (§26.5), so exhaustion — not only error — loses; and out-of-protocol sponsorship (§25.1) makes exposure profitable where a payer exists. The owner's counterargument that this is maximal distribution rather than exploitation is right about emission and does not answer the procedure: reputation during selection is an assumption, not a right. Candidate levers already inside the design vocabulary: pay the first challenger from the collected sanction pool instead of from the position above, credit sponsorship for unrepresented low-XP accusers, and cap removals at the ladder's early depths where F13 shows Council intervention restores accuracy. Any lever must be shown not to make frivolous challenges profitable (F6's harassment boundary).

**A7 — ANALYSIS, price stabilization sits exactly on a D32 falsifier.** The owner proposes Treasury D buyback as the mechanism that stabilizes price over time. D32 states the thesis fails if retention depends only on assumed token prices or subsidies rather than demonstrated usefulness. Retention supported by external buyback capital therefore belongs to the falsifier side until tested: compare retained participation with and without bucket-D support under declared valuations. Already covered by the Atmo calibration item's subsidy-dependence acceptance in [BACKLOG.md](../BACKLOG.md); recorded here so the tension is not forgotten, without opening a second mandate.

**A8 — ANALYSIS from source inspection, with diagnostic software checks, 2026-10-24: equal one-day maturities remove the legacy fixture's own-XP timing premium under an earliest-use comparison.** `src/incentives.mjs` pins 72/168-hour maturity, zero invitation freeze and immediate acceptance in its legacy scenario; `FacetyModel` currently requires 24-hour spend, conversion and invitation freeze by default. For the separately implemented [P23 counterpart](incentives.en.md#one-day-counterpart-contract-p23), a sponsor act recorded at 0 h is terminally disposed at 24 h both by earliest invitation and earliest waiting conversion, so the old day-3 versus day-7 own-XP timing difference cannot be inherited. Admission then waits until 48 h; a fresh newborn act at 48 h can convert at 72 h. Equal-hour admission/work need journal order, not invented sub-hour clocks or a D34 legal-event choice. The pre-data reporting design therefore retains both snapshots and does not estimate work-at-admission by an `atHour <= 48` filter, which would wrongly include later same-hour identity work. This conditional API/timing deduction is now checked by the [eight one-day software regression families and exact legacy preservation controls](../studies/2026-10-24-one-day-incentives/README.md), not a new empirical/mechanism claim, calibrated payoff, actual preference or retention finding. F45 now retains freshly reconstructed diagnostic tables/thresholds without copying or reclassifying historical outputs; expected future losses remain unknown despite no scripted debt/verdicts. The bounded one-day port/report is complete; the existing guarantee-selection/alignment owners retain the stronger choice and liability questions. No new reward/liability rule or general attack/benefit result is selected.

## Comparative analysis: Facety and Bitcoin

| Aspect | Bitcoin | Facety |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What secures it | Physical cost: energy and hardware (proof of work) | Social cost: daily fresh presence, guarantees with liability, public scrutiny |
| Sybil resistance | One unit of hashpower, one vote; identity irrelevant | One person, one identity; identity is the point |
| Unit of value | Scarce coin (21M cap, halvings) | Emission per active person per day: supply grows with the population; burns and buyback counteract |
| Main asset | The coin itself | The trust graph and XP (who is a real, accountable person); FACY is the distribution mechanism |
| Trust assumption | Honest majority of hashpower | Hypothesized honest Council majority and denser honest network; no established global person-fraction economic guarantee (D62) |
| Privacy | Pseudonymous | Deliberately public and accountable |
| What it enables | Neutral, censorship-resistant money and settlement | One-person-one-vote, Sybil-resistant distribution, reputation, human verification in a world of AI agents |

**Analysis hypothesis A1 (not a calibrated economic finding or owner-selected monetary rule):** higher FACY value may increase fake-identity incentives while human attention/collection capacity need not scale automatically. The response of sanctions, investigator compensation, fabrication technology, useful services and Treasury D policy needs a separate conditional model. A guaranteed inverse price/security relation, guaranteed application value, and a policy that sanctions must scale with price do not follow. The Bitcoin analogy is explanatory, not empirical validation of Facety.

## Method lessons

- Random scripted runs cannot model incentives. Use strategy menus, payoff facts and equilibrium or deviation analysis ([games](games.en.md)).
- A strategy menu that is too small misleads: adding `farm-and-harass` reversed the apparent safety of inaccurate verdicts.
- Check valuation traps: debt left on abandoned fakes is not a cost to the attacker (`falseDebt: +1`).
- Exploratory candidate counts are not discoveries (228 candidates, two of them confirmed).
- Confirm on reserved seeds before calling anything a mechanism; record what was not tested.
