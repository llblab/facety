# Simulator contract

The simulator is one Rust crate, [`simulator/`](../simulator), standard library only (D130). It replaced the Node.js simulator; that implementation, its tests and its raw results are preserved only at the git tag `legacy-node`. This page owns what is implemented, the declared deviations from the [protocol](facety.protocol.en.md), every simulation assumption, and how the baseline was validated. Protocol meaning lives in the protocol; decisions in the [decision log](decisions.en.md); findings in the [insight register](insights.en.md); open work in [BACKLOG](../BACKLOG.md).

**Role under conditional-mainnet preparation (D188):** close the executable selected subset, make a possible future launch measurable, and supply registered conditional decision surfaces without claiming human calibration. No rehearsal or pilot precedes mainnet development under this direction. D168 remains REVISE; neither model outcomes nor this contract authorize deployment or accept launch risk without human evidence. OPEN admission, financial, migration, ordering and privacy contracts still require owner choices. The [canonical plan](../BACKLOG.md#conditional-mainnet-preparation) owns confirmation and work order; no new mechanics, kernel extraction or research result is claimed here.

See the [conformance map](#rule-conformance-mapping) for coverage and gaps, and the [Self-ID](#self-id-transaction-fixture) and [appeal](#appeal-fixture-contract) sections for trusted fixture APIs.

**Currency arithmetic (D184/D185):** `Amount` stores unsigned `u128` quanta of 10^-12 FCTY; signed net accounting uses `i128` quanta. Stakes use the exact rational nominal formula, then one final upward rounding to a quantum. No intermediate rounded stake is compounded. Money never passes through floating point; approximate conversions serve ratios and plots only. Configuration `moneyPolicy = integer-quanta/12-decimals/final-stake-ceiling/v1` participates in the digest. Saved earlier whole-FCTY results retain their original arithmetic and bytes; they do not validate this later executable.

## Layout and use

**Ticker compatibility (D172).** FCTY is the current monetary ticker; FACY was its former name. This is one asset with unchanged units and mechanics, not a conversion or a new balance. Current prose and generated SVG captions use FCTY. Saved JSON/JSONL evidence, archived figures and the historical prolongation source remain byte-for-byte as recorded. The `result-v4` field `acquired.heldFacy` retains its serialized name for compatibility and denotes FCTY amounts; historical `transferFacy`, stake fact and event identifiers are also preserved when describing their original APIs. The rename changes no configuration, randomness or numerical result and requires no result-schema migration or new experiment.

With `--out DIR` each seed writes `DIR/<seed>-<configuration digest>.json` containing `meta` (build commit, dirty flag, elapsed seconds, threads, command line) and `result` (schema `facety-sim/result-v10`, engine `facety-sim/runner-v14`, randomness policy, initial/full phase/effective configurations, completed days, censor reason, counters and daily history). The digest covers the seed, initial configuration and phases, including money/admission policies, observation dependence, Council-halving ablation, Council and appeal execution/voting policies, and randomness identity. Existing files require explicit `--overwrite`. `tests/cli.rs` guards saved files and deterministic results. V10 adds `voluntaryCorrectionPolicy` to recorded configurations and their digest. V9 replaces `openingTransactions.unfundedAtMaturity` with `openingTransactions.refusedUnfunded` (filings refused at recording for lack of spendable Collector FCTY, also counted in `detection.casesRefused`); a v8 `unfundedAtMaturity` value is not comparable to it. V8 added `openingTransactions.unfundedAtMaturity` and removed the active-cancellation and moot counters. V7 added exact decimal money, `openingTransactions` and `economics.pendingOpenings`; `economics.openCases` counts active cases only, while legacy `detection.casesOpened` counts accepted filings. V6 added `restoration`: execution policy, cases reversed, original units restored, outstanding debt cancelled and blocked conditional appeals. V5 added `councilRounds`; V4 added cohort debt, held/debtor account-days, ongoing cases, absolute holdings and acquired-account resources. `debtByCohort` remains historical created debt; use `economics.outstandingDebt` for current debt and `restoration.debtCancelled` for write-offs. Debt creation, repayment and cancellation remain separate history. Monetary JSON numbers are exact FCTY decimal text; consumers must use decimal arithmetic to retain every quantum. Settled-case hold hours can double-count overlapping holds; `heldAccountDays` counts their daily union and includes ongoing cases. These day counters are discrete samples, not sub-day duration measurements. Endpoints are the last sampled day before the declared horizon, never an extrapolation.

Observation sensitivity flags: `--repeat-correlation P` and `--persistent-errors`. Default remains fresh independent observations, correlation 0. No repeat model is a protocol admission rule. The simulator retains its nominal stake ceiling of 4,294,967,295 FCTY, now expressed in quanta; larger stakes are explicitly refused as a model bound, not a protocol cap, and Council entry still works. Exact rational arithmetic uses a bounded 512-bit intermediate fallback beyond `u128`, checked against an independent decimal-digit reference; the finer quantum does not shrink the nominal ceiling.

**Engine history:** `runner-v14` adds direct voluntary correction, and activity excludes invalid acts from the recording window. The population runner does not generate voluntary correction commands. `runner-v13` excludes active accused Self-ID terminals and evaluates invitation eligibility against live topology inputs, matching the freshness rule of Self-ID. `runner-v12` enforces the distinct Self-ID terminal: the author's own Council seat no longer counts. `runner-v11` computes Self-ID reachability and layered support from live links, accepted status and dispute exclusions at the scheduled Council and difficulty snapshot. It can no longer admit an issuer on a same-hour cut, held intermediary or stale support level. The schema and randomness policy are unchanged. `runner-v10` removed the former extra condition for Council-member guarantors: the two-node path from the emitter to a member-guarantor is accepted without the former condition that the seat reach another member, so emitters whose only passing guarantor is a Council seat are no longer refused. This is a rule-conformance repair that changes growth and issuance outcomes, not an outcome-preserving refactor; the removed condition had also constrained emitters near isolated seats (see [Activity and paths](#deviations-and-assumptions)). `runner-v9` reserves the first stake at filing (D199): an unfunded challenger is refused at recording instead of holding the accused's slot for 24 hours, and activation reuses the reservation. The scheduler now credits maturities before an event hour, then activates, then credits that hour's maturities; this ordering is outcome-equivalent to the removed activation-candidate boundary. `runner-v8` limits stake custody to liquid FCTY (D198). `runner-v7` adopts the D197 dispute lifecycle and release-time credit; case-admission and Council execution policy changes are included in configuration digests. `runner-v6` adds D196's suspended disputed maturity; `runner-v5` invalidated source-distance caches when the eligibility snapshot changes, matching a fresh path search; the earlier stale cache could reject newly available paths on an unchanged graph. This is a correctness repair that can change outcomes, not an outcome-preserving efficiency claim. Independent brute-force and certificate checks remain in force. Seed, genesis and World mechanics cannot change through `begin_phase`; such changes are refused atomically rather than recorded as if the existing World had adopted them. CLI preflight rejects overflowing seed ranges, slot steps, horizon/defense clocks and invalid shock inputs. A day-zero shock applies before the first attack step; long patience cannot wrap into premature invitations. Standalone stdout summaries now include engine and randomness policy as well as seed/digest. Randomness stays v3. Frozen results retain their original builds and bytes. The [previous contract](https://github.com/llblab/facety/blob/8a9d0f52739b03c35a4b769a628d46edeefa70e0/docs/simulator.en.md) preserves earlier fixture and build comparisons; no broad remeasurement is claimed.

## Appeal fixture contract

The public types are exported from `world`. `request_appeal(prior_case, affected)` accepts only a settled case and an account with a recorded affected role, receipt or debt; invalid status and lost keys do not by themselves bar requesting reconsideration. A request starts no clock and supplies no verdict or restored assets.

World retains each original `case_outcome`: actual settlement time, positions, Council rounds/electorates, transfers and debt creation, plus any explicitly supplied trusted proof/evaluator winner override. Appeal requests recognize ordinary participants and actual charged guarantors even when they have no unpaid debt or XP penalty. Reversal preserves the original outcome; its effects journal records corrections separately.

`record_appeal_opening_fixture(request, initiator)` is explicitly a **core-only admitted test fixture**, with policy `trusted-core-only/canonical-opening/recording-xp/24h/council-rounds/no-financial-execution/v2` (`APPEAL_FIXTURE_POLICY`). It requires an unreversed settled case with an actual final `OpeningRecord`; the already-active `begin_case` fixture cannot fabricate that transaction. A participant must be an accepted usable account with strictly greater XP than the prior opening's author. The actual accused must have no unresolved dispute. Both XP values and the new author's event-time mutual guarantors are retained at recording. The prior opening separately retains the challenged author's own historical roster; this is not the old identity case's XP-recipient proxy. `TrustedCollectiveCouncil` means collective authorization has been supplied externally to the fixture, never that one member has acquired unilateral authority. Evidence acceptance and signature verification are not implemented.

`record_transaction_appeal_fixture(request, target, initiator)` is the typed-target form of that same fixture. The target must be exactly `TransactionTarget::Opening(request.prior_case)`; a Self-ID/invitation with the same number or another opening is refused before allocating an appeal ID, custody, holds or effects. The original entrypoint delegates through this check. `AppealRecord::target()` derives this typed link from the retained prior case rather than storing a second mutable copy. This adds neither a production authority nor independent Self-ID/invitation dispute admission; the fixture policy and financial omissions are unchanged.

After 24 hours, `tick` activates the appeal and its first seven-day Council round, snapshots membership, restricts the opening's ordinary author if any and the prior accuser, and freezes the latter's unspent rights and unprocessed Self-ID maturities. Accepted recording is not revalidated against later XP/status changes. Every appeal is its own referendum; it has no ordinary escalation positions. The shared scheduler completes the activation/maturity batch before taking the membership snapshot for new rounds (see [maturity ordering](#suspended-self-id-maturity)). This submission-order convention and first-ballot-per-member policy are simulator assumptions, not selected production ordering or revision contracts.

`appeal_ballot` binds appeal, member and round. Its public choices name the prior verdict explicitly: confirm, reverse and restore, or defer. Retained generic Council rounds encode these as `Confirm`, `Oppose` and `Defer` respectively. The equal-ballot majority calculation is shared with the ordinary ladder. Only the cutoff recognizes the result; no majority automatically defers seven days, snapshots membership again and carries no ballots forward. Deferral creates no stake, sanction, winner or restoration. There is no modeled deferral cap.

`resolve_appeal` executes a recognized result and then releases only that appeal's restrictions, deferred maturities and slot. Confirmation applies no new sanction. Reversal invokes case-specific restoration; a completed-key-migration blocker retains reversal authorization, holds and the slot, without partial restoration. The blocked execution is journaled and may be retried. If a separately authorized restoration already ran, execution reports `AlreadyRestored`. Completed resolution replays return the retained result without repeating effects.

No appeal stake amount, custody, new monetary sanction, opening-guarantor merits penalty or transferred-money refund is chosen. This omission is the fixture boundary, **not a zero-fee protocol default**. A participant opening permits free pending cancellation by its author or trusted recording-time guarantor. Active withdrawal refuses independently of financial scope. A collective opening cannot be cancelled by an individual. Fee-free honest-link repair remains separately trusted and checks accepted status, activity holds, solvency, current slots and absence of an existing link/valence. New emission still passes the ordinary live support/path gate. There is no automatic link reconstruction or catch-up issuance.

Evidence: [`tests/appeal.rs`](../simulator/tests/appeal.rs) exercises wrongful-sanction restoration end to end, historical receipts, debt, no asset duplication, same-accused refusal across case types and deferred review, pending cancellation, active cancellation refusal, replay/cutoff boundaries, new electorates and batched versus hourly time. Child-module tests cover retained reversal authority after completed recovery, atomic refusal, corrupt records and invalid penalty fractions. Existing World tests cover overlapping revocations, FIFO repayment/cancellation, later integrity repairs/losses, original recipients and debt-suspended missing credits. These are deterministic transition checks, not human or production validation.

## Voluntary correction

`World::correct_own_self_id` applies [voluntary correction](facety.protocol.en.md#voluntary-correction) under `direct-author/one-act/two-funded-components/replay-integrity/v1`. Typed `CorrectionInput` binds a trusted authority premise to the synthetic network, correction version, author, target and nonce; missing or wrong scope, lost keys, an unknown target, a different author, replay or unmatured acts refuse before mutation. This fixture has no production signature verifier or persistent-key migration. If any affected receipt has a retired legacy recipient, the operation refuses rather than mutating or duplicating credit.

The World checks external priority and free Collector funds before execution. An earlier pending identity case, an applicable asset freeze or an earlier uncancelled Self-ID case whose frozen scope names the act blocks the operation; cancelling a pending filing releases its priority. A separately reserved stake remains in place. Burn unspent backing plus 1 FCTY from Collector, or 2 FCTY from Collector if backing was previously disposed; record separate amounts in an immutable `SelfIdCorrection`. No case, debt, reward or reversal of a dependent payment is created.

`self_id_history` and `self_id_chronology` retain original IDs, recording hours and predecessor references, with an explicit invalid position. `valid_self_ids` derives the traversal without rewriting historical links. `has_valid_self_id_at` tests the recording window under current validity, not historical admission or authority. The model keeps no signed payloads or media commitments; the projection can be reconstructed from its ordered records but cannot independently authenticate production signatures, captures or proofs. Receipt processing numbers let integrity replay omit just the target's own repair, including on an empty receipt. Receipt causes are independent of stake, disposition and each other.

[`correction_tests.rs`](../simulator/src/correction_tests.rs) checks 32-act daily and monthly traces, the exact gap and original successor reference, burns under each source disposition, exact and insufficient funding, preserved reservations and causes, prior filings and frozen scope, cancellation, equality between incremental and batched execution and rejection without state changes. These are deterministic software checks. The population runner does not model voluntary behavior.

## Self-ID transaction fixture

The public types are exported from `world`. Policy `trusted-core-only/typed-self-id-target/filing-frozen-n/liquid-reserved-at-filing/prior-correction-exclusion/v6` (`SELF_ID_CASE_POLICY`); v5 reserved the frozen-`N` first stake at recording (D199), and v6 omits already voluntarily corrected acts from a newly filed scope. This is the engineering subset authorized by D193 on the [Q25-A1 boundary](../DECISIONS_NEEDED.md#q25-a1-first-self-id-fixture-trust-boundary), now executing the D189 amounts on the filing-frozen scope selected by D195. It is not production admission, authentication, collective authority, a general asset-priority rule or an appeal contract.

`record_self_id_case_fixture(accuser, target, premises)` accepts a typed `TransactionTarget::SelfId(act)` only; an invitation, opening or coincident numeric ID refuses before any record allocation. Admission and historical-duty qualification are `TrustedPremise` fields that must be `Supplied`; the merits are a separate `TrustedMerits` value at settlement. No evaluator label or caller allegation is treated as a verdict. Ordinary non-author eligibility is still the selected recording-time gate: both accounts accepted and distinct, the accuser's XP strictly above the challenged owner's, a live uncancelled source that was not already corrected, a free accused-subject slot and no previously settled correction of that target in this fixture. Role conflicts that would make the case unexecutable — the opener appearing in the challenged act's creation roster, a duplicate roster entry, an unknown roster account — refuse at recording, while the case owns no record, custody or hold; multiple-role overlap beyond that remains OPEN (Q25-B1).

Filing fixes the disputed scope: `violating` (the 1-based historical position), the inclusive `endpoint` (the accused's chain length at filing) and the list of affected acts, omitting prior voluntary corrections. Its length is `n`. It also reserves all `n` FCTY from spendable Collector funds and occupies the slot in the same transition. An unfunded attempt is refused without recording. At 24 hours activate the holds and clock on that reservation, without repricing or another funding check. `cancel_self_id_case_opening` follows the ordinary pending cancellation contract, releasing all reserved funds and the slot once without deciding merits. A later admissible filing is fresh.

Self-IDs recorded after the filing are outside that case: they keep their own receipts and rights, cannot raise its stake, charge or guarantor charges, and correcting them requires a separate accusation with its own filing-time snapshot. Later verdicts and Council deferrals also cannot reprice the frozen count.

`self_id_case_plan(case)` is the read-only effect plan: `Ok` reports the frozen affected suffix, the frozen charge, what is collectible now and the resulting unpaid amount; `Err` names why the case cannot settle at this moment. A refusal is a capability or OPEN-contract boundary, never a verdict, expiry, acquittal or zero-valued settlement. Plan and settlement share one predicate set, so a previewed plan is what execution commits.

`settle_self_id_case_fixture(case, merits)` executes that plan in one World-owned effect path. It requires the baseline stake/halving/integrity settings (no ablation), one ordinary accuser with no later ordinary or Council entry, the ordinary cutoff reached, and a frozen scope consistent with the accused's recorded chain. Asset state is a plan input, not an admissibility test: a spent, transferred, burned, reserved or otherwise unavailable amount lowers actual collection and raises this case's debt instead of stranding an activated case. `TrustedMerits::NotUpheldSupplied` refuses, including on replay of a settled case.

Effects, in order: collect the accused's actual backing for the first accuser under the model's sanction collection order (spendable liquid, then mature own rights; the uncollected remainder is this case's FIFO debt); revoke exactly the frozen scope's actual receipts under a case-keyed cause; halve the losing author's integrity once under the declared integrity convention; charge each original creation-time guarantor its own frozen `n` and burn the actual collection, recording its shortfall as that guarantor's own debt; release the accuser's own reserved backing and this case's restrictions only; and journal the result. The identity status and the valid personal prefix are preserved, and no assets outside the payer's own holdings are taken, so no case-local priority is introduced. Calling an identity verdict, a role-specific identity penalty or an appeal request on this case kind refuses.

The isolated D192 witness (`n = 1`, the accused holding only the target's mature unspent right) is the special case of this path: collection transfers 1 FCTY to the accuser, source burn and unpaid charge stay zero, and the guarantor charges burn 1 FCTY each. It is an outcome of the generic rule under those premises, not a separate priority rule.

The journal (`SelfIdSettlement`) retains the target, the frozen affected suffix and endpoint/`n`, original and charged rosters, actual collection/award, source burn, unpaid amount, guarantor charge and burn, every revoked receipt with its credit time and actual recipients, and integrity before and after. A repeated settlement with the upheld premise returns the retained journal without a second payment, burn, penalty or receipt removal. The structural audit pins the journal's full accounting and the monetary/lifecycle counters. `restore_self_id_case_effects` reuses the case-specific restoration primitive: it returns only this case's receipt units and penalty event, replays integrity without that loss, cancels only this case's unpaid debt, keeps prior burns and transfers (no refund rule is selected), does not recreate the consumed right, and preserves unrelated cases and independent causes.

Boundaries. A completed key recovery that already moved receipts or liability refuses before any effect and is recorded as a named OPEN contract (Q25-C1), not as a verdict or expiry. Value transferred away before settlement is not recovered from third parties; the shortfall is debt (transferred-money refunds stay OPEN). Repeat-enforcement identity across sequential cases and unavailable-value recovery remain OPEN. The fixture models only supplied upheld merits at cutoff, not ordinary escalation, Council entry or an acquittal for this transaction kind. An unsupported settlement does not release the holds or slot.

Evidence: [`tests/self_id_case.rs`](../simulator/tests/self_id_case.rs) covers the executed isolated path, the filing-frozen deep scope (`n` above one with actual collection and recorded debt), additional Self-IDs during the pendency, a later act that needs its own case, disposition changes before activation, disposition changes inside the pendency, an unreserved versus reserved backing path, guarantor shortfall, overlapping cases, key recovery, identity/role/appeal refusals, typed-target, alias and role-conflict refusals, missing premises, non-upheld merits, free pending cancellation, pre-cutoff refusal, departed original guarantors against a changed graph, batched versus stepwise time, replay and case-specific reversal. The event-log verifier's prologue executes an isolated case and a filing-frozen deep case with a converted target and a cancelled out-of-scope act, then reverses both. These are deterministic software checks, not human or production validation.

## Suspended Self-ID maturity

[Suspension](facety.protocol.en.md#suspension) applies to unprocessed Self-IDs of the accused, not to other participants. The model uses this whole-hour order:

- `World::tick` credits maturities strictly before the event hour. At the hour it activates due ordinary and Self-ID openings in case-ID order on their existing reservations, then establishes due appeal holds in appeal-ID order. It then processes the hour's maturities: defer unprocessed acts held for an accused and credit unaffected acts at their scheduled hour. The cursor skips deferred acts so other accounts continue.
- Refresh membership and process ordinary cutoffs and appeal rounds in their respective ID order. Calls at that hour run afterwards in call order; cancellation of a pending opening must be strictly before `matures_at`, and a new filing sees only free Collector FCTY. A jump processes each due boundary, not one snapshot from the endpoint.
- Suspended acts yield no usable right, units, repair or debt repayment. Eligible cancellation removes the pending entry; `cancel_self_id` refuses a suspended target of a pending or active typed case, an authorized case source or an already corrected source. These guards do not decide broader financial precedence.
- At closing, the authorized result applies before the case's maturity hold is removed. An adverse identity verdict applies the accused's chain cut within `settle_case`; a trusted Self-ID correction invalidates affected uncredited acts before release. If a legacy spend age already disposed of an invalidated source, suppress its unissued credit without rewriting the asset's disposition. A confirmed or executed appeal releases its accused only after applying its recognized outcome. Closing processes only uncredited and now-unheld maturities already due; future maturity stays scheduled. The source's `Credit.at` is the actual credit time (including empty debt receipts), and the scheduled time is derived from the original act. Case-linked causes are released separately; another participation hold is not a release or an extension of this one.
- The audit reconstructs slots, reservations and holds from retained records, rejects credited/invalid/unheld deferred entries, verifies scheduled ≤ receipt time ≤ present, compares late receipts with the release counter, and requires an actual credit by the time a source funds an invitation. No suspended/deferred diagnostic counters from these paths rewrite historical evidence.

Evidence: [`tests/suspension.rs`](../simulator/tests/suspension.rs) checks activation ordering, delayed release time and replay through extensions, invalidation before credit, unaffected accounts, authorized cancellation and jumps versus stepwise time. The event-log verifier compares the slot, hold, deferred set and actual receipt after every event.

## Randomness

Every decision draws from its own stream keyed by (root seed, channel, actor, period), so unrelated decisions never share a stream offset. Generator: splitmix64 seeding into xoshiro256**. In `facety-sim-rng/v3`, Council channel 21 uses actor `(case_id << 32) | member_id` and period `round_number`; each member/round has a separate turnout/belief draw. Other channels keep v2's actor/day keys. V2 added channel 22 for optional persistent target errors, retaining v1's channels 1-21. V1 replaced the retired `sha256-xoshiro128starstar-v2`; Rust and Node outcomes are statistically comparable, not bit-identical. A new key layout or generator needs a new identifier. A snapshot clones the whole simulation state (`Sim: Clone`).

## Rule conformance mapping

The [protocol](facety.protocol.en.md) owns the rules. This map records what Rust executes, where to check it, and where the model stops.

| [Artifacts and claims](facety.protocol.en.md#artifacts) | `create_self_id` records an abstract act after the support, path, participation and rolling-slot checks. | No media registration, signed face claims or publication checks. |

### Transaction responsibility records

Rust retains distinct facts rather than deriving every duty from the current graph or from a funding source:

| Transaction | Retained facts | Execution boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-ID | `Act::subject`, recording time, immutable creation-time guarantors and actual `Credit.at` and `Credit.recipients` (possibly empty or revoked) | Source revocation and identity-case correction exist; only the [trusted Self-ID subset](#self-id-transaction-fixture) executes separate Self-ID transaction merits |
| Invitation | `Invitation::creator`, issuance time, `funding_act` and immutable issuance-time `guarantors` | The roster records accepted mutual partners, without an activity filter; it is not an adjudicated liable set or the funding Self-ID's roster. No invitation XP credit or merits penalty is created |
| Opening | `OpeningRecord` author/accused, recording-time XP, mutual roster and lifecycle timestamps | Ordinary lifecycle and linked appeal fixtures exist; opening-specific merits sanctions remain OPEN |

The model captures the invitation roster after its issuance checks and records it with the funding act when consuming the right. They survive veto, acceptance, link termination and later graph changes. The funding Self-ID's snapshot and actual receipts remain attached to that Self-ID; spending it earns/revokes no XP. `invitation_provenance_survives_roster_changes_veto_and_link_termination` separates the funding, issuance and later rosters and checks foreign-funding refusal before economic mutation. `audit_rejects_invalid_invitation_funding_and_roster_provenance` checks structural history integrity: a distinct own mature consumed funding source and sorted, distinct, non-self, in-range roster IDs. The audit does not reconstruct historical membership from today's graph or authenticate a supplied history.

`World::transaction_provenance(TransactionTarget)` exposes borrowed records for `SelfId`, `Invitation` and `Opening` without mutation or cross-kind/identity fallback. Opening IDs address the ordinary-case opening table, not every protocol transaction or appeal-opening record; active fixtures without an opening record are absent. These are model-local tagged IDs, not a selected production encoding or authenticated ledger commitment. The view preserves the transaction author/time, its own guarantors, a separately typed funding link and actual XP receipt time/recipients/revocation state. A pending, suspended or pre-credit cancelled Self-ID has no receipt, a debt-suspended maturity has an explicit empty receipt, and a revoked receipt retains its original recipients. Non-Self-ID transactions do not inherit source receipts. Missing funding/receipt fields describe recorded data, not fee-free admission or zero financial obligations.

`typed_transaction_provenance_separates_namespaces_and_actual_receipts` resolves coincident IDs to distinct authors and checks missing, issued and revoked receipts; the debt-service regression checks explicit empty receipts. `typed_appeal_targets_reject_aliases_and_unrelated_openings_before_mutation` checks rejected links against balances, XP, restrictions, counters, records and ID allocation, and compares valid typed/compatibility fixture records. These focused checks are not the full-state fingerprint/differential kernel proof planned in B.

These records are not production fault or liable-party qualification, authenticated veto or admission. General repeat enforcement across case classes and unavailable-emission recovery outside [voluntary correction](#voluntary-correction) remain open. No independent invitation or opening merits execution is inferred from retained facts.

**Ordinary identity model boundary.** `OpeningRecord` retains author and accused but no typed challenged transaction. `settle_case` performs identity-case settlement and the authorized accused chain cut together, using the opening stake and historical actual-receipt roster. The runner can separately supply a D127 related-identity correction or trusted role-specific penalty. These are not substitutes for the Self-ID fixture, transaction-level production admission, or an appeal executor.

## Deviations and assumptions

- **Invitations and slots.** `create_invitation(creator, act)` records `target: None` and counts only the creator's slot. `accept_invitation` creates the recipient account, binds it and counts its slot. There is no initial Self-ID or recipient capacity check at issuance. Cancellation releases only the inviter's pending slot; termination of an accepted invitation releases both. The model does not invite an existing unsupported subject or let the new guarantor pay for a returning subject's valence. See [Slots](facety.protocol.en.md#slots) and [Invitations](facety.protocol.en.md#invitations).
- **Activity and paths.** An accepted account counts as active if it recorded a noncancelled, noninvalidated Self-ID in the past 28 days; activity does not wait for credit or verify personhood. The active count's digit length stands in for difficulty. Council and difficulty refresh on ticks and due round cutoffs. Both issuance gates use that roster and difficulty with live links, status, holds and support. The shared path cache keys on the graph revision and rules and is invalidated by institutional snapshots, path holds and status changes.

  A Self-ID needs a current mutual guarantor on a path of at most `max_nodes` nodes including the emitter and a different Council member as terminal. A member-guarantor can terminate the two-node path; another first hop is an intermediate and has at most `max_nodes - 2` links left. The issuer does not need a support level. For a member issuer the search omits their seat as a terminal and never traverses the issuer. Otherwise shared reachability suffices: removing a loop through the issuer yields a shorter route via another first hop. Unlike the invitation family search, this single reachability check does not use the invitation work budget.

  An actively accused member cannot terminate a path, including the direct Self-ID path. The separate invitation search returns a work-exhaustion refusal when it cannot settle the question within its budget. `--self-path-from-guarantor` counts from the guarantor and does not require its support level. `--exclude-challengers` also excludes challengers; `--allow-disputed` removes dispute-based path exclusions. These are ablations, not reference rules.
- **Integrity precision.** The model stores integrity in millionths. One is 1,000,000 and a valid own maturity adds 1,000 up to that cap. Each penalty stores `floor(coef * (1 - fraction))` through the existing `f64` fraction path. Replay uses the same rounding. XP is `floor(units * coef / 1,000,000)`. Seven halvings store 7,812 rather than 7,812.5 millionths; at 128 units the grid gives 0 XP and exact arithmetic gives 1. Policy `integrityPolicy = millionths/penalty-f64-floor/own-maturity-1000/cap-one/v1` identifies the default in configurations and summaries; earlier outputs use the same arithmetic without the label. In `integrity.rs`, `grid_penalty` and `grid_repair` reuse that default. The other reference, `candidate/exact-rational-halving/own-maturity-1000/cap-one/final-floor/v1`, stops at `TooManyHalvings` or `TermOverflow` (60 halvings, 120-bit terms), with no fallback. The candidate comparison does not change configuration, digest or schema. See `tests/integrity_precision.rs` and the open [precision choice](../DECISIONS_NEEDED.md#q26-integrity-precision-candidate).
- **Money.** Self-ID backing uses minted escrow; cancellation burns it. Stakes reserve only free Collector FCTY. Other sanctions, balance burns and transfers cannot take this reservation; cutting its source cannot remove withdrawn money. The model's separate merits collection takes spendable liquid first and then mature own rights. Fractional collection or debt repayment converts an entire right and leaves exact liquid change. Repayment burns actual backing rather than paying a past winner. The [nominal stake ceiling](#layout-and-use) is a model bound, not a protocol cap.
- **Admission and Council execution.** Case admission policy `trusted-ids/recording-roster/single-accused/liquid-reserved-at-filing/v5` participates in the digest. An unfunded filing counts in `detection.casesRefused` and `openingTransactions.refusedUnfunded`; it is not cancellation or merits loss. The runner has no pending cancellation probability and removes a cancelled filing before choosing new targets. Council execution policy `trusted-ids/first-ballot/activation-aware-maturity-before-round/daily-entry-cache/v2` appears as `councilExecutionPolicy` and as `councilRounds.executionPolicy`; it and the voting policy participate in the digest. Trusted IDs stand in for authentication. The first timely ballot per member is retained, with no revisions. Round numbers start at zero. Initial ordinary entry uses the cached Council; subsequent snapshots follow the [scheduler](#suspended-self-id-maturity), with active electorates fixed between rounds. The ladder keeps ballots and deadline provenance and World keeps the corresponding electorates; settlement returns both. Saved population counts aggregate rounds, not case evidence.
- **Evidence and human behavior.** Evaluator labels (cohort and identity kind) reach only observation and verdict models, never World. Supplied probabilities for looks, error dependence, willingness and service are uncalibrated. There is no knowledge test for understanding the rules. The runner's daily limit of one ordinary join per case is not an eligibility condition. Its optimistic outside-proof path lets genuine accused accounts, not fabricated or second accounts, prove reality with a supplied probability and delay. An accepted proof overrides the ladder only after ordinary cutoff or a recognized final Council result and proof completion. Ordinary entry stays closed while waiting; neither a pending nor deferred vote can be bypassed. Attacker operators are real people; only cone and second accounts are false. Fake accounts have no extra cost beyond protocol FCTY in the model. Detection stays on in attack phases. Report outcomes only conditional on scenario, policy, horizon, seeds and criterion.

### Restoration effects

The case-keyed primitive rejects missing, open or previously reversed cases. It removes only this case's status and source-revocation causes and restores only its original `Credit.recipients` once no other cause blocks them. Empty debt receipts and pre-credit cancellations yield nothing. It neither recreates consumed rights nor refunds burns or transfers. Links, other cases' holds and unrelated effects remain. The journal includes optional inviter penalties and the separately invalidated related account when the runner supplies those effects. `restoration.statusesRestored` counts actual status changes; `xpHalved` and `low_coef` retain historical penalties and minimum integrity.

Integrity replay keeps original maturity counts between loss events (including capped and debt-suspended repairs), later losses and inherited key-recovery caps. For the regression from 1 through a loss, 300 repairs and another loss (ending at 0.4), reversal of the first loss replays to 0.5, not 0.8. The flat-balance ablation returns the recorded cut without repricing other cuts. Tranches retain original, unpaid, repaid and cancelled amounts by case; reversal cancels only the case's unpaid remainder, not cumulative debt creation or repayment. Completed key recovery blocks restoration before mutation: routing the correction to a successor and updating an inherited cap need their own contract.

The population `--appeal` option supplies a conditional approved reversal, not a filed Council appeal. It carries the original case ID and uses `conditional-approved-case-reversal/earned-receipts/integrity-replay/fifo-debt/v1` in `appealExecutionPolicy` and `restoration.executionPolicy`. A migration blocker increments `blockedConditionalAppeals`; it does not restore a subject. The [core appeal fixture](#appeal-fixture-contract) separately tests linkage, review and execution, with no claim about human availability.

## Verification surface

**Second certificate verifier (`certificate.rs`).** It answers *is this supplied family valid?*, never *does one exist?*. It restates the specification's predicates with its own data structures and reports one named failure: `TooFewPaths`, `PathTooShort`, `PathTooLong`, `WrongSource`, `UnknownNode`, `MissingEdge`, `NotSimple`, `BlockedIntermediate`, `SharedVertex`, `ReusedEndpoint`, `InvalidTerminal`, `DuplicateRoute`, `UnknownSource`, `DimensionMismatch`. It shares no acceptance logic with `paths::verify_family`. Tests compare both verifiers on the same families, confirm every successful exact-search family passes both, compare the exact search with an independent brute-force existence reference on decidable small graphs, and check each malformed variant. A rejected certificate never implies that no alternative family exists, and `Search::Exhausted` stays distinct from `Search::Insufficient`: issuance refuses for lack of a certificate, not proven infeasibility.

**Event-log verification (`event_log.rs`, test-only).** Fixed seeds (11/29/47), 240 events, six accounts and a shrink budget of 400. After every event it runs the structural audit (money conservation, exclusive custody, FIFO debt, XP reconstructed from receipts and integrity history, slot accounting) plus an explicit supply/liquid identity and a custody exclusivity check. Rejected operations must leave the declared comparison projection identical. The projection covers every protocol-semantic field; excluded are only `stats` (declared diagnostic counters) and the path-search, Self-ID reachability and `Arc` work caches (efficiency state, D145). On every seed the prologue executes the isolated and filing-frozen deep Self-ID cases and their reversals, and checks funded filing, refusal, cancellation and activation. Failures print the recorded seed and a greedily reduced trace and are meant to become named regression tests.

**Declared before/after panel (finite engineering control).** Three representative configurations — baseline 10 attackers/60 days, the `--no-layered` ablation at 60 days, and `--accuse 1 --appeal 1` with 6 attackers/40 days — on seeds 1 and 2, one thread, fixed horizons and no reserved research seeds. Earlier checkpoints used the same panel as an equivalence check for outcome-preserving work (the D193 fixture, the verifiers, the `world.rs` decomposition), where the `result` payloads were byte-identical with build/run metadata outside `result`.

For D196 the panel is deliberately a divergence check, because suspending disputed maturities changes the mechanics. Observed: the baseline configuration reproduces its previous payload exactly apart from the engine identifier, the `--accuse 1 --appeal 1` configuration keeps its dispute counts and its daily history while a few money/civil counters move (`attackerLiquid` 1 → 0, `debtCreated` 483 → 484 on seed 1) because the extended cancellation window and the suspended credits shift individual events, and the `--no-layered` ablation moves materially (settled cases and false invalidations 259 → 186 on seed 1, 59 → 145 on seed 2) because deferred maturity changes when the accused's units arrive. These differences are the intended effect of the selected rule, not a defect: no fixture was adjusted to hide them, and the schema, randomness policy, configuration digests and frozen evidence are unchanged. Frozen results keep their original engine identifier and are not revalidated by this panel.

For runner-v10 the panel is again a divergence check, because accepting the two-node member-guarantor path changes growth and issuance. Observed: the `--no-layered` ablation reproduces its previous payloads exactly apart from the engine identifier on both seeds, while the layered baseline and the `--accuse 1 --appeal 1` configuration diverge from the first affected acceptance onward. The snapshot for seed 2 arrives at day 159 instead of 190 in both layered configurations, changing the phase configuration digest (completed days: 250 → 219 and 230 → 199 respectively), honest emission rises (baseline seed 1: 133,093 → 135,245 units) and honest Council-path refusals move with the shifted difficulty trajectory in both directions (baseline seed 2: 5,319 → 2,349; baseline seed 1: 1,008 → 1,329). The mechanism is the removed member-reachability condition, not a fixture adjustment; the schema, randomness policy and frozen evidence are unchanged, and frozen results keep their original engine identifier.

The four dispute refactors between `4048f63` and `02b144d` match all six complete `result` payloads byte-for-byte. The runner-v11 freshness fix changes the outcome of the same-hour deterministic graph witnesses, but all six runner-v10 → runner-v11 panel payloads match byte-for-byte after replacing only the explicit engine identifier. The distinct-terminal rule in runner-v12 rejects the isolated member/nonmember pair when the member emits, but permits a member to emit via supported intermediaries to another seat. All six runner-v11 → runner-v12 panel payloads also match except for the engine identifier. These are engineering controls, not revalidation of historical studies.

**Defect found by that verifier and fixed.** Unknown invitation, valence, act or case identifiers and out-of-range account identifiers panicked in several public entry points instead of refusing (`accept_invitation`, `cancel_invitation`, `end_invitation_link`, `withdraw_valence`, `convert_to_collector`, `open_valence`/`create_invitation` through their maturity check, `end_case`, `probe_invitation_paths`, and the trusted `penalize_xp`/`burn_liquid`/`invalidate_identity`/`reinstate_identity`/`void_chain` primitives). They now return an explicit `Reject`, `Probe` refusal or no-op, persisted as the named regression `unknown_identifiers_refuse_instead_of_panicking`. This changes no accepted-path outcome, schema or saved result.
