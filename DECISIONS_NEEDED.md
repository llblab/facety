### Q16 — D50 falsifier design (D108)

[D108](docs/decisions.en.md#evidence-core-convergence-d76) released the master gates; the [D50 falsifier](studies/2026-10-29-d50-falsifier/README.md) needs these pre-data bindings. **Bound by D109 (2026-10-29): the owner accepted all six defaults.**

### Q24 — Halving of losing participants against the stability of the Council (D159, registered result)

Answered 2026-11-07: **D164**. The halving stays for everyone, no verification or second confirmer is added; the problem is the simulator's examiner model (single noisy look, no cost awareness). The original question follows.

Blocking consequence, measured on reserved seeds (study of 2026-11-07, claims H1 to H3): with the selected ladder and the baseline detection, honest growth in a one-year attack-free control falls from about 40 thousand accounts to about 12.6 thousand because losing participants lose half their XP, and the high-XP Council members, who can challenge anyone and so open the most cases, are halved out of the XP-ranked Council (Council seats changing hands: about 310 a year against about 110 without halving). Smallest question: is the loss of half the XP of a losing accuser intended to apply to an accuser whose case was opened on a noisy look, or should a formal case need independent verification (Q20 option b) before it can cost anyone XP? ### Q23 — Assumptions taken in the convergence of 2026-11-07 (D162, D163)

Answered 2026-11-08: **D166** (items 1, 4, 5, 6, 7) and **D167** (items 2 and 3, the debt ceiling, D70-D72, D34 and the Treasury). Nothing from this list is open. The original list follows.

Taken by the builder to implement the selected rules without inventing more than necessary; each is declared in the [simulator contract](docs/simulator.en.md#deviations-and-assumptions) and the owner may overrule any of them. Where an item blocks a claim, the exact consequence is named.

1. **Who stands for the accused when the accused has less XP than the accuser.** The case gate requires the accuser to hold more XP than the accused, and an entrant needs more XP than the last participant, so the accused cannot be position 2. The simulator lets other accounts (mutual partners with enough XP and funds) stand for the accused, and keeps the cheap proof of reality (D136) as a separate process that decides for the accused. Question: may the accused enter as the first defender without the XP condition? Consequence: without this, a genuine person without a senior friend can only rely on the proof of reality.
2. **Boundary of a deadline.** The deadline is a cutoff: anyone eligible can join until it, nothing is added to it (answered, D167).
3. **Council entry is one terminal position** on the same clock with the window of its ordinal; ballots never add positions or move the deadline; a tie or an empty vote leaves the side of the entering member; a stake that would go to a Council entry is burned because no recipient contract is selected (D64). Consequence: this settles who receives the stake of a loser beneath the Council only provisionally.
4. **Stake backing and rounding.** A stake is exclusive custody of liquid FCTY (no emission-balance debit, no unsecured commitment), in whole FCTY with the ceiling of `7 * 1.2^(n-1)`. A sanction that cannot be collected from spendable liquid FCTY becomes recorded debt and is never collected later. XP halving removes the floor of half the balance. Consequence: realized losses of attackers are underestimated when they hold little liquid FCTY; the debt column shows the shortfall. Question: may a matured entitlement left on the Emitter account back a stake? With liquid backing only, a population that never withdraws (`--keep-rights 1000`) opens no dispute at all, which switches detection off.
5. **Charges to the accused's guarantors** use the accused's mutual guarantors at the moment the case opens (act-time snapshots of D55 do not exist for an identity case).
6. **Examiner care** (`examiner_looks` 2) and **joining behavior** (`join_rate` 0.1 per day, `council_rate` 0.5 per day once contested, `council_turnout` 0.7, supporters' beliefs correct with the accuracy 0.9, attackers entering on the last day for their side) is not a protocol rule and has no calibration. Consequence: durations and the escalation depth are conditional on it; panels vary it and the findings say so.
7. **XP after consumption and revocation.** Cancelling an invitation inside its freeze does not revoke the matured XP of the source Self-ID (D163), and the D9 revocation of already credited receipts when the ending party leaves a guarantee is kept for credited receipts only. Question: should a guarantor who leaves keep XP already credited for a relation's past acts?

### Q22 — Bindings of the escalating stake ladder (D159)

Answered 2026-11-06: **D159**, **D158** and **D161**. A losing accuser's stake goes to the first participant who stood for the accused; the ladder is an ordered list (odd positions on one side, even on the other) with each stake 20% above the previous one, compounding, so every entrant claims about 83% of its own stake; the base stake is 7 FCTY, charged to the accused's emission balance, which may go negative, and the same amount to each guarantor of the accused, the guarantors' charges being burned; stakes are reserved and released at the end, a loser's stake going to the next position; the window shortens by one day per joining participant from seven days to a floor of three; any Council member may join any dispute, after which the whole Council votes on whether the first Council member who joined is right, with no stake required. The simulator does not model stakes or joining.

### Q21 — Requirements for the implementation specification (D155)

Answered 2026-11-06: (a) the proof window by **D158** (the wait shortens from seven toward three days as people join); (b) the tolerance for damage to honest people by **D160** (no hard limit; the simulator counts; the owner's rough scale is under about 100 to 200 wrongly lost disputes, depending on network size); (c) the cost of a false accusation by **D159** (escalating stakes). The original question follows.

The closure programme found three normative choices that the simulator cannot make for the owner. (a) **A bound on the time an accused person has to prove reality.** The honest network falls to 83% of the control with a 14-day proof window and to 56% with a 28-day window (30 operators, one year; the defense itself is unaffected). Options: state a maximum case duration as a protocol requirement, or leave it open and accept that the network size depends on how slow outside verification is. (b) **A tolerance for damage to honest people** (cases on genuine people per honest account-year, wrongly invalidated people per year, time to recover). The simulator reports these against a paired attack-free control but assigns no limit. (c) **Whether a false accusation costs the accuser.** Today an accuser pays only the loss of its own activity while its case is open; false cases end in a wrongly invalidated person about once in 200, so the harm is linear in the number of false cases (158 people a year at three false cases per operator and day with 30 operators). Options: a stake or a rate limit per accuser, or accept the bounded harm.

### Q20 — Verification before a formal case opens (D153)

Answered 2026-11-06: **D157**, the softer D131 (option d) is the default; verification before a case stays an open research lever (`--confirm`, `--confirm-public`). The original question follows.

Measured: false cases against genuine people (each a seven-day hold, and paths avoid disputed accounts, D131) are a main limit on honest growth in the baseline; one independent confirming look before a case opens cuts them from about 3,000 to about 20 a year and the honest network grows about four times larger with the same attacker share. Options: (a) keep the current rule and accept the limit; (b) require independent confirmation by honest examiners other than the observer, public evidence counting (the variant that keeps detection in a fake cluster); (c) confirmation by the target's own partners (rejected by the measurements: fake clusters have none); (d) soften D131 so that only the accused party of a case, not the challenger, is excluded from paths (measured: 31,068 honest accounts instead of 10,373, cone containment unchanged; both parties keep the activity hold). Runner status: `detection.confirm`, `detection.confirm_public`, `world.exclude_targets_only` (`--targets-only`), all default off.
