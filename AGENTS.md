## Surfaces and ownership

- **English only, no prompt quotes.** The repository contains no Russian text and no literal quotes of owner prompts, voice notes or chat messages. Restate every owner decision as a rule or a research choice in plain English.
- [Protocol](docs/facety.protocol.en.md) owns current meaning. Revise the owning rule directly; keep conditions, recorded facts, effects, timing, refusal and exceptions together. Use short, consistent names. Keep history, owner attribution, examples from named studies, APIs and tasks out of the normative text. Link to rules by descriptive section name, not by a decision ID that readers must resolve.
- [Rules of the Game](docs/rules-of-the-game.en.md) explains the same rules for participants. Keep it coherent, free of decision history and simulator settings, and in step with changed protocol rules. It does not settle open contracts.
- [Simulator contract](docs/simulator.en.md) owns the implemented subset and deviations. Map behavior to the owning protocol section. Defaults follow selected rules; label every necessary unselected simulation assumption in the contract and output. Trusted fixtures and evaluator truth supply neither production authority nor verified independent people. Raw guarantee counts use stored incoming edges.
- BACKLOG alone owns tasks; CHANGELOG records milestones; [DECISIONS_NEEDED](DECISIONS_NEEDED.md) keeps questions and assumptions; studies retain their own evidence. The [decision log](docs/decisions.en.md) records ID, date, status, paraphrase and what changed for decisions. Record each insight with source and evidence level (confirmed, exploratory, diagnostic), and put follow-up in BACKLOG. Research-only choices belong with the decisions and studies, not protocol rules.
- **Periodic consolidation (D132, D139).** Consolidate at a milestone, when a reader can no longer scan the backlog or findings in one sitting, or when requested. Inventory new decisions, findings and questions. Classify individual clauses: keep selected meaning in its owning rule, findings at their actual evidence level, alternatives and unresolved choices with their evidence or questions, and dated material in a pinned archive. Before removing text, check that every surviving rule and useful concept has a home and every new normative statement has a basis. Fold before deleting; repair historical links against the original build or document snapshot. Run project checks and record the milestone in CHANGELOG; do not turn an audit checklist into another ledger.
- **Phases (D140/D141).** Until every baseline axis has a named default in the simulator contract and BACKLOG, prefer hypotheses, feedback and targeted checks over exhaustive sweeps. Prioritize stability and predictability, then analysis, understanding and representativeness. Only after the baseline is fixed does a broad benchmark make sense. BACKLOG states the current phase.

## Research boundaries

- **Conditional-mainnet preparation (D188).** The simulator closes the executable subset, prepares launch measurement and compares candidate rules under explicit unmeasured human assumptions. No rehearsal or pilot precedes mainnet development under this direction; neither is authorized. D168 stays REVISE, not PROCEED. Development, code tests and synthetic results never authorize deployment or accept launch risk without human evidence; that remains a separate explicit owner decision. Normative OPEN contracts require proposals and owner choices, with at most one focused question per checkpoint. Kernel extraction needs its own owner checkpoint; instrumentation must precede pallet code. No new external dependencies, session, recruitment or personal-data collection.
- **Registration before every new research run (D188).** Commit the question, exact arms/configurations, reserved seeds, resource budget, stop rules and kill criteria before execution; use fresh output directories and never overwrite evidence. No matrix expansion after an unfavorable result. Corrupt-examiner attacks or adversarial-search red teams need owner-declared behavior and budget first. Deterministic transition/property/fuzz regression tests are software verification, not human calibration or a substitute for research registration. Unpriced account value, link prices and harm tolerances remain reported axes, not assigned constants.
- Strategic lens: D200 purpose, a voluntary decentralized registry of people who have lived (registry-office authority over reality and rule compliance only, no moral verdict; human factors set aside in this phase); D32 accountability for farming-resistant emission and growth; D49 structure must earn its growth cost; D50 10× asymmetry is a target, not a property; D62 51%/two-thirds are conditional questions, not thresholds; D68 core-first human-operated defense; D53 no price floor from finite issuance; D76 minimum causal evidence over more mechanisms, longitudinal over static.
- **Attack simulations always run with detection on.** Without detection nothing opposes a cluster of fakes, so a no-detection arm is self-evident and carries no information about a real network. Never launch, propose or report a no-detection attack arm as evidence. The open research question is how detection is designed (looks, vetoes, leaving, public evidence, escalation, verdict accuracy, false-positive cost), and that is what to vary. Existing no-detection (`gate`-only) results stay in the tree as labeled legacy baselines (D115, D125), not as findings to extend.
- Keep evaluator ground truth outside protocol state (`World` never sees cohort or identity labels); defense consumes declared observations only. Hidden identity/control labels never reach defense.
- Separate uniqueness, contribution/reputation and distribution; XP and traceability are never verified usefulness or truth. Keep effort, realizable net profit and actual recovery distinct; debt is not a burn; zero/negative/missing denominators stay honest.
- Report attack probabilities only conditional on scenario, policy, horizon, seed sampling and success criterion, with uncertainty and real recovered costs.
- Preserve missingness: endpoints come from the declared horizon; zero total XP has `null` concentration.
- Incentive analyses separate recorded consequences, supplied private utility and justified externalities; a max over scripts is not an equilibrium; report valuation sweeps and individual deviations.
- Mechanism claims need a committed registration (presence, necessity by ablation, named alternatives, a kill criterion), written before running, on reserved seeds. The Rust simulator has no registration tooling yet; until it does, every result is diagnostic. Exploratory thresholds are not mechanisms; ablations are counterfactuals, not protocol options.
- D37/D38: v2 registrations bind `family-contradiction/v1`; v1 keeps its digest and statistics. Drift permission is not method switching; new-method analysis of old data is a separate artifact.
- D108 (2026-10-29) released the master-programme order lock and the WP1/G1 method precondition; every claim still needs a committed registration, kill criterion and reserved-seed confirmation, and real/personal data needs explicit license/ethics approval.

## Engineering

- The monetary token ticker is **FCTY**, expanded as **FaCT You** with that capitalization and spacing. The former ticker FACY is retained only in naming history, immutable pre-rename evidence and compatible serialized/historical API identifiers; do not rewrite saved evidence or break its schema for an editorial rename.

- Research runners and evidence generation use Rust. Do not add or use Python helpers for project work, and do not revive Node.js as a research runner; the Node simulator stays retired.

- Reproducibility: seeds and the randomness policy identifier (`facety-sim-rng/v3`; v1/v2 for historical runs) are part of every result; a new key layout or generator needs a new identifier. V3 keys each Council ballot by case, member and round, retaining v2's other channels. Randomness is decision-keyed: unrelated actions never couple through stream offsets. Scheduler or data-structure optimizations must not change outcomes for a given seed unless declared as a deviation in the [simulator contract](docs/simulator.en.md).
- **Two baselines (D145).** The mechanics baseline (rules and baseline axes) defines results. The efficiency baseline (search order, caches, data layout, scheduling) must not change outcomes for a given seed: a mechanism that is faster simply becomes the efficiency default, and the change is checked by identical saved results before and after. When an optimization can change outcomes only through a declared channel (a search that runs out of its work budget), declare that channel in the simulator contract and report the counter. Results record the efficiency settings in force.
- Compare optimized path algorithms with the brute-force reference and the independent verifier before measuring performance (`paths.rs` tests).
- Delete verified dead code without asking; check consumers first.
- Success criterion for the topology defense (D129): dirty-FCTY share at most 10% on the longest declared horizon; a clean share below two thirds fails the scenario.
- On every commit, from `simulator/`, run `cargo fmt --check`, `cargo clippy --release --all-targets -- -D warnings` and `cargo test --release`. The tests include the documentation integrity guard (links, anchors, English-only text); do not weaken it.
- Commit scoped changes at validated checkpoints and push to the configured upstream under the normal workflow unless the task is local-only. No force-push, history rewrite or unrelated changes. Every commit that completes a milestone (a rule adopted, a baseline axis fixed, a study closed, a simulator capability or fix of note) also adds its CHANGELOG entry in the same commit; check the CHANGELOG before committing.
